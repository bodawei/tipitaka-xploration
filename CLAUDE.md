## Pali Doctrinal Glossary

Core doctrinal terms as used in the Verañjakaṇḍa (Vinaya, Pārājikapāḷi §§1–20). Definitions reflect this text's usage; other contexts may differ.

| Pali | English | Sense in this text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| dhamma | teaching; phenomenon, state | Both the Buddha's teaching and, in the plural (`akusalā dhammā`), mental states |
| brahmacariya | the holy life | Not just celibacy — here the whole Dispensation, which does or doesn't endure (§§18–20) |
| avijjā | ignorance | The shell enclosing beings (`avijjaṇḍakosa`); destroyed in the first watch |
| vijjā | true knowledge | The three knowledges won in the three watches of the night |
| āsava | taint, canker, influx | Three: sensuality (`kāmāsava`), becoming (`bhavāsava`), ignorance (`avijjāsava`). Their destruction is arahantship |
| rāga, dosa, moha | greed, hatred, delusion | The three roots; the Buddha "annihilates" these, hence `ucchedavāda` |
| kusala / akusala | wholesome / unwholesome | The basic ethical division of mental states |
| duccarita / sucarita | misconduct / good conduct | Threefold by body, speech, and mind (`kāya-`, `vacī-`, `mano-`) |
| kamma | action, deed | Seen operating in `yathākammūpaga`, beings faring on according to deeds |
| diṭṭhi | view | `Micchādiṭṭhi` (wrong view) leads down, `sammādiṭṭhi` (right view) up |
| jhāna | meditative absorption | The four, given in full formula in §11, as the basis for the three knowledges |
| samādhi | concentration | `Samāhitaṃ cittaṃ ekaggaṃ`, the collected, one-pointed mind |
| sati | mindfulness | Paired with `sampajañña` (clear comprehension) in the third jhāna |
| upekkhā | equanimity | Culminating in the fourth jhāna's purity of mindfulness |
| pīti / sukha | rapture / pleasure | Factors of the first two jhānas |
| dukkha | suffering, unsatisfactoriness | First of the four truths, known `yathābhūtaṃ` (as it really is) |
| samudaya | origin, arising | Second truth: the origin of suffering |
| nirodha | cessation | Third truth |
| paṭipadā | path, way of practice | `Dukkhanirodhagāminī paṭipadā`, the way to cessation |
| vimutti | liberation | The mind released from the taints, with the knowledge that it is released |
| saṃsāra (implied) | round of rebirth | Seen in the recollection of past lives and in `punabbhavābhinibbatti`, rebirth in a new existence |
| jāti | birth | `Khīṇā jāti` — "birth is destroyed" |
| upādāna | clinging | `Anupādāya`, "without clinging," the mode of the monks' liberation in §19 |
| kappa | aeon, world-cycle | Contracting and expanding, the scale of the first knowledge |
| sīla (implied by) sikkhāpada | training rule | The rules whose absence caused earlier Dispensations to vanish |
| pātimokkha | the monastic code | Recited or not recited — the second factor in a teaching's survival |
| vinaya | discipline | Punned on in `venayika`: the Buddha "disciplines" the defilements |
| saṅgha | community | The third refuge, taken by Verañja |
| saraṇa | refuge | Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha |
| pariyāya | manner of speaking, indirect sense | The device structuring §§3–10: a slur accepted only when redefined |
| nibbāna (implied by) ucchinnamūla, tālāvatthukata | cut off at the root, made like a palm stump | The stock formula for defilements permanently incapable of arising again |

### Notes

- `pariyāya` and `brahmacariya` carry the most weight: the first drives the opening dialogue, the second sets up the argument for why a Vinaya is needed at all.
- `sutta` puns on "thread" and "discourse" in §§19–20. Flowers scattered because they are `suttena asaṅgahitāni` ("not held together by a thread") stand for a teaching that vanishes because it was never gathered into suttas and a code of rules.
- Preserve diacritics (ā ī ū ṃ ṅ ñ ṭ ḍ ṇ ḷ) when quoting Pali. Long vowels and retroflexes are lexically distinctive.
