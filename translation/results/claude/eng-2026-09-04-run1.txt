Pārājikapāḷi — 1. Pārājikakaṇḍaṃ

[64] Monks who were enemies brought a monk into the presence of a human woman and pressed him down with his sexual organ onto her anus … her vagina … her mouth — when he was covered and she uncovered, when he was uncovered and she covered, when both were covered, when both were uncovered. If he consented to the insertion, consented to it having entered, consented to it remaining there, consented to its withdrawal, there is an offence entailing defeat … but if he did not consent, there is no offence. Monks who were enemies brought a monk into the presence of a human woman who was awake … asleep … drunk … insane … heedless … dead but not yet gnawed by animals … dead and mostly not gnawed … there is an offence entailing defeat. They brought him into the presence of one dead and mostly gnawed and pressed him down with his sexual organ onto her anus … her vagina … her mouth — when he was covered and she uncovered, when he was uncovered and she covered, when both were covered, when both were uncovered. If he consented to the insertion, consented to it having entered, consented to it remaining, consented to its withdrawal, there is a grave offence … but if he did not consent, there is no offence. Monks who were enemies brought a monk into the presence of a non-human woman … a female animal … a human hermaphrodite … a non-human hermaphrodite … an animal hermaphrodite … a human paṇḍaka … a non-human paṇḍaka … an animal paṇḍaka … a human man … a non-human man … a male animal, and pressed him down with his sexual organ onto the anus … the mouth — when he was covered and the other uncovered, when he was uncovered and the other covered, when both were covered, when both were uncovered. If he consented to the insertion, consented to it having entered, consented to it remaining, consented to its withdrawal, there is an offence entailing defeat … but if he did not consent, there is no offence.

[65] Monks who were enemies brought a monk into the presence of a male animal that was awake … asleep … drunk … insane … heedless … dead but not gnawed … dead and mostly not gnawed … there is an offence entailing defeat. They brought him into the presence of one dead and mostly gnawed and pressed him down with his sexual organ onto the anus … the mouth — when he was covered and the other uncovered, when he was uncovered and the other covered, when both were covered, when both were uncovered. If he consented to the insertion, consented to it having entered, consented to it remaining, consented to its withdrawal, there is a grave offence … but if he did not consent, there is no offence. Just as the case of enemy monks has been set out in full, so should the rest be set out in full: enemy kings … enemy thieves … enemy rogues … enemy libertines (the "lotus-scented"). In brief.

[66] If he inserts an organ into an orifice, there is an offence entailing defeat. If he inserts an organ into what is not an orifice, there is an offence entailing defeat. If he inserts what is not an organ into an orifice, there is an offence entailing defeat. If he inserts what is not an organ into what is not an orifice, there is a grave offence. A monk misconducts himself with a sleeping monk: if, on waking, the latter consents, both are to be expelled; if, on waking, he does not consent, only the violator is to be expelled. A monk misconducts himself with a sleeping novice: if, on waking, the latter consents, both are to be expelled; if he does not consent, only the violator is to be expelled. A novice misconducts himself with a sleeping monk: if, on waking, the latter consents, both are to be expelled; if he does not consent, only the violator is to be expelled. A novice misconducts himself with a sleeping novice: if, on waking, the latter consents, both are to be expelled; if he does not consent, only the violator is to be expelled. There is no offence for one who does not know, for one who does not consent, for one who is insane, for one deranged in mind, for one overwhelmed by pain, and for the first offender.

[67] At that time a certain monk engaged in sexual intercourse with a female monkey. He became remorseful: "The Blessed One has laid down a training rule; can it be that I have committed an offence entailing defeat?" He reported the matter to the Blessed One. "Monk, you have committed an offence entailing defeat." At that time a number of Vajjian monks of Vesālī engaged in sexual intercourse without having renounced the training and without having declared their weakness. They became remorseful: "The Blessed One has laid down a training rule; can it be that we have committed an offence entailing defeat?" They reported the matter to the Blessed One. "Monks, you have committed an offence entailing defeat." At that time a certain monk, thinking, "In this way there will be no offence for me," engaged in sexual intercourse dressed as a layman. He became remorseful: "The Blessed One has laid down a training rule; can it be that I have committed an offence entailing defeat?" He reported the matter to the Blessed One. "Monk, you have committed an offence entailing defeat." At that time a certain monk, thinking, "In this way there will be no offence for me," engaged in sexual intercourse naked. He became remorseful … "Monk, you have committed an offence entailing defeat." At that time a certain monk, thinking, "In this way there will be no offence for me," engaged in sexual intercourse wearing a garment of kusa grass … a garment of bark … a garment of wood shavings … a blanket of human hair … a blanket of horsehair … a covering of owls' wings … an antelope hide. He became remorseful … "Monk, you have committed an offence entailing defeat." At that time a certain almsgoing monk saw a little girl lying on a small couch, and, filled with lust, he inserted his thumb into her private parts. She died. He became remorseful … "Monk, there is no offence entailing defeat; it is an offence entailing a formal meeting of the Sangha."

[68] At that time a certain young brahmin was infatuated with the nun Uppalavaṇṇā. Then, while the nun Uppalavaṇṇā had entered the village for alms, that young brahmin entered her hut and hid himself there. The nun Uppalavaṇṇā, returning after the meal from her almsround, washed her feet, entered the hut, and sat down on the small bed. Then that young brahmin seized the nun Uppalavaṇṇā and raped her. The nun Uppalavaṇṇā told the nuns about this matter. The nuns told the monks. The monks reported the matter to the Blessed One. "Monks, there is no offence for one who does not consent."

[69] At that time the characteristics of a woman appeared in a certain monk. They reported the matter to the Blessed One. "Monks, I allow that very same preceptor, that very same ordination, and those very same years [of seniority] to be transferred to the nuns' community. Those offences of monks that are shared in common with nuns are to be cleared in the presence of the nuns; as for those offences of monks that are not shared in common with nuns, for those offences there is no offence." At that time the characteristics of a man appeared in a certain nun. They reported the matter to the Blessed One. "Monks, I allow that very same preceptor, that very same ordination, and those very same years [of seniority] to be transferred to the monks' community. Those offences of nuns that are shared in common with monks are to be cleared in the presence of the monks; as for those offences of nuns that are not shared in common with monks, for those offences there is no offence."

[70] At that time a certain monk, thinking, "In this way there will be no offence for me," engaged in sexual intercourse with his mother … engaged in sexual intercourse with his daughter … engaged in sexual intercourse with his sister. He became remorseful … "Monk, you have committed an offence entailing defeat." At that time a certain monk engaged in sexual intercourse with his former wife. He became remorseful … "Monk, you have committed an offence entailing defeat."

[71] At that time a certain monk had a supple back. Oppressed by dissatisfaction, he took his own sexual organ in his mouth. He became remorseful … "Monk, you have committed an offence entailing defeat." At that time a certain monk had a pendulous organ. Oppressed by dissatisfaction, he inserted his own sexual organ into his own anus. He became remorseful … "Monk, you have committed an offence entailing defeat." At that time a certain monk saw a corpse, and on that corpse there was a wound near the genitals. Thinking, "In this way there will be no offence for me," he inserted his sexual organ into the genitals and drew it out through the wound. He became remorseful … "Monk, you have committed an offence entailing defeat." At that time a certain monk saw a corpse, and on that corpse there was a wound near the genitals. Thinking, "In this way there will be no offence for me," he inserted his sexual organ into the wound and drew it out through the genitals. He became remorseful … "Monk, you have committed an offence entailing defeat." At that time a certain monk, filled with lust, touched with his sexual organ the private parts of a clay figure. He became remorseful … "Monk, there is no offence entailing defeat; it is an offence of wrongdoing." At that time a certain monk, filled with lust, touched with his sexual organ the private parts of a wooden doll. He became remorseful … "Monk, there is no offence entailing defeat; it is an offence of wrongdoing."

[72] At that time a monk named Sundara, who had gone forth from Rājagaha, was going along a carriage road. A certain woman said, "Wait a moment, venerable sir, I will pay respects," and while paying respects she lifted up his inner robe and took his sexual organ in her mouth. He became remorseful … "Monk, did you consent?" "I did not consent, Blessed One." "Monk, there is no offence for one who does not consent." At that time a certain woman, seeing a monk, said to him: "Come, venerable sir, engage in sexual intercourse." "Enough, sister, that is not allowable." "Come, venerable sir, I will make the effort, you make no effort; in this way there will be no offence for you." That monk did so. He became remorseful … "Monk, you have committed an offence entailing defeat." At that time a certain woman, seeing a monk, said to him: "Come, venerable sir, engage in sexual intercourse." "Enough, sister, that is not allowable." "Come, venerable sir, you make the effort, I will make no effort; in this way there will be no offence for you." That monk did so. He became remorseful … "Monk, you have committed an offence entailing defeat." At that time a certain woman, seeing a monk, said to him: "Come, venerable sir, engage in sexual intercourse." "Enough, sister, that is not allowable." "Come, venerable sir, insert it inside and release outside … insert it outside and release inside; in this way there will be no offence for you." That monk did so. He became remorseful … "Monk, you have committed an offence entailing defeat."

[73] At that time a certain monk went to a charnel ground, saw a corpse that had not been gnawed, and engaged in sexual intercourse with it. He became remorseful … "Monk, you have committed an offence entailing defeat." At that time a certain monk went to a charnel ground, saw a corpse that was mostly not gnawed, and engaged in sexual intercourse with it. He became remorseful … "Monk, you have committed an offence entailing defeat." At that time a certain monk went to a charnel ground, saw a corpse that was mostly gnawed, and engaged in sexual intercourse with it. He became remorseful … "Monk, there is no offence entailing defeat; it is a grave offence." At that time a certain monk went to a charnel ground, saw a severed head, and, touching the open mouth, inserted his sexual organ. He became remorseful … "Monk, you have committed an offence entailing defeat." At that time a certain monk went to a charnel ground, saw a severed head, and inserted his sexual organ into the open mouth without touching it. He became remorseful … "Monk, there is no offence entailing defeat; it is an offence of wrongdoing." At that time a certain monk was infatuated with a certain woman. She died and was discarded in a cemetery, and her bones lay scattered about. Then that monk went to the charnel ground, gathered the bones together, and applied his sexual organ to the place of the private parts. He became remorseful … "Monk, there is no offence entailing defeat; it is an offence of wrongdoing." At that time a certain monk engaged in sexual intercourse with a female nāga … with a female yakkha … with a female ghost … with a paṇḍaka. He became remorseful … "Monk, you have committed an offence entailing defeat." At that time a certain monk had damaged faculties. Thinking, "I feel neither pleasure nor pain, so there will be no offence for me," he engaged in sexual intercourse. They reported the matter to the Blessed One. "Monks, whether that foolish man felt anything or did not feel anything, it is an offence entailing defeat." At that time a certain monk, thinking, "I shall engage in sexual intercourse with a woman," felt remorse as soon as he had touched her. He became remorseful … "Monk, there is no offence entailing defeat; it is an offence entailing a formal meeting of the Sangha."

[74] At that time a certain monk at Bhaddiya, in the Jātiyā Grove, had gone for the day's abiding and was lying down. His limbs became stiff with wind. A certain woman saw him, sat down upon his sexual organ, took her pleasure as she wished, and departed. The monks, seeing him soiled, reported the matter to the Blessed One. "Monks, in five ways the sexual organ becomes erect: through lust, through faeces, through urine, through wind, and through being bitten by a caterpillar. In these five ways, monks, the sexual organ becomes erect. It is impossible, monks, it cannot happen, that this monk's organ was erect through lust. That monk is an arahant. There is no offence, monks, for that monk." At that time a certain monk at Sāvatthī, in the Blind Men's Grove, had gone for the day's abiding and was lying down. A certain cowherd woman saw him and sat down upon his sexual organ. That monk consented to the insertion, consented to it having entered, consented to it remaining, consented to its withdrawal. He became remorseful … "Monk, you have committed an offence entailing defeat." At that time a certain monk at Sāvatthī, in the Blind Men's Grove, had gone for the day's abiding and was lying down. A certain goatherd woman saw him … a certain firewood-gatherer saw him … a certain cow-dung gatherer saw him and sat down upon his sexual organ. That monk consented to the insertion, consented to it having entered, consented to it remaining, consented to its withdrawal. He became remorseful … "Monk, you have committed an offence entailing defeat."

[75] At that time a certain monk at Vesālī, in the Great Wood, had gone for the day's abiding and was lying down. A certain woman saw him, sat down upon his sexual organ, took her pleasure as she wished, and then stood nearby laughing. That monk woke up and said to that woman: "Is this your doing?" "Yes, it is my doing." He became remorseful … "Monk, did you consent?" "I did not know of it, Blessed One." "Monk, there is no offence for one who does not know."

[76] At that time a certain monk at Vesālī, in the Great Wood, had gone for the day's abiding and was lying down leaning against a tree. A certain woman saw him and sat down upon his sexual organ. That monk got up at once. He became remorseful … "Monk, did you consent?" "I did not consent, Blessed One." "Monk, there is no offence for one who does not consent." At that time a certain monk at Vesālī, in the Great Wood, had gone for the day's abiding and was lying down leaning against a tree. A certain woman saw him and sat down upon his sexual organ. That monk kicked her off and threw her down. He became remorseful … "Monk, did you consent?" "I did not consent, Blessed One." "Monk, there is no offence for one who does not consent."

[77] At that time a certain monk at Vesālī, in the Great Wood, in the Hall with the Peaked Roof, had gone for the day's abiding and was lying down with the door open. His limbs became stiff with wind. Now at that time a number of women came to the monastery bringing perfume and garlands, wishing to look at the dwellings. Then those women, seeing that monk, sat down upon his sexual organ, took their pleasure as they wished, and saying, "What a bull of a man!" they laid the perfume and garlands upon him and departed. The monks, seeing him soiled, reported the matter to the Blessed One. "Monks, in five ways the sexual organ becomes erect: through lust, through faeces, through urine, through wind, and through being bitten by a caterpillar. In these five ways, monks, the sexual organ becomes erect. It is impossible, monks, it cannot happen, that this monk's organ was erect through lust. That monk is an arahant. There is no offence, monks, for that monk. Monks, I allow one who goes into seclusion by day to close the door before going into seclusion."

[78] At that time a certain monk of Bhārukaccha, having engaged in sexual intercourse with his former wife in a dream, thought, "I am not an ascetic, I shall disrobe," and while going to Bhārukaccha he met the venerable Upāli on the way and told him the matter. The venerable Upāli said: "Friend, there is no offence for what occurs in a dream." At that time in Rājagaha there was a laywoman named Supabbā of naive faith. "Enough, sister, that is not allowable." That monk did so. He became remorseful … "Monk, there is no offence entailing defeat; it is an offence entailing a formal meeting of the Sangha."
