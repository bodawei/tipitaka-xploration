Pārājikapāḷi — 1. Pārājikakaṇḍaṃ
vin01m.mul.xml, verses 142-151

[142] Now at that time a certain bhikkhu, during a famine, entered a rice-house and with thievish mind carried off a bowlful of boiled rice. He became remorseful… (and so on) … "You, bhikkhu, have committed an offence entailing defeat." Now at that time a certain bhikkhu, during a famine, entered a butcher's house and with thievish mind carried off a bowlful of meat. He became remorseful… (and so on) … "You, bhikkhu, have committed an offence entailing defeat." Now at that time a certain bhikkhu, during a famine, entered a cake-house and with thievish mind carried off a bowlful of cakes… (and so on) … with thievish mind carried off a bowlful of crisp sweetmeats… (and so on) … with thievish mind carried off a bowlful of sugar-balls. He became remorseful… (and so on) … "You, bhikkhu, have committed an offence entailing defeat."

[143] Now at that time a certain bhikkhu, seeing an article by day, made a mental note: "I will carry it off by night." Taking it to be that article, he carried off that same one… (and so on) … taking it to be that article, he carried off another… (and so on) … taking it to be another, he carried off that one… (and so on) … taking it to be another, he carried off another. He became remorseful… (and so on) … "You, bhikkhu, have committed an offence entailing defeat." Now at that time a certain bhikkhu, seeing an article by day, made a mental note: "I will carry it off by night." Taking it to be that article, he carried off his own article. He became remorseful… (and so on) … "There is no offence entailing defeat, bhikkhu; there is an offence of wrong-doing."

[144] Now at that time a certain bhikkhu, seeing a purse on a stool, thought: "If I take it from here I shall be one defeated," and so he moved it away together with the stool and took it. He became remorseful… (and so on) … "You, bhikkhu, have committed an offence entailing defeat." Now at that time a certain bhikkhu with thievish mind carried off a mattress belonging to the Saṅgha. He became remorseful… (and so on) … "You, bhikkhu, have committed an offence entailing defeat."

[145] Now at that time a certain bhikkhu with thievish mind carried off a robe from a robe-rack. He became remorseful… (and so on) … "You, bhikkhu, have committed an offence entailing defeat." Now at that time a certain bhikkhu, having taken a robe inside a dwelling, thought: "If I go out from here I shall be one defeated," and did not go out of the dwelling… (and so on) … They reported this matter to the Blessed One. "Whether that foolish man went out or did not go out, bhikkhus, there is an offence entailing defeat."

[146] Now at that time two bhikkhus were companions. One bhikkhu entered the village for alms. When solid food was being distributed by the Saṅgha, the second bhikkhu took his companion's portion and, in reliance on their intimacy, ate it. When the other found out, he reproached him: "You are no recluse!" He became remorseful… (and so on) … "With what mind did you act, bhikkhu?" "It was taking on trust, Blessed One." "There is no offence, bhikkhu, in taking on trust."

[147] Now at that time several bhikkhus were doing robe-work. When solid food was being distributed by the Saṅgha, the portions of all of them were brought and set down nearby. A certain bhikkhu, taking another bhikkhu's portion to be his own, ate it. When the other found out, he reproached him: "You are no recluse!" He became remorseful… (and so on) … "With what mind did you act, bhikkhu?" "I perceived it as my own, Blessed One." "There is no offence, bhikkhu, for one who perceives it as his own." Now at that time several bhikkhus were doing robe-work. When solid food was being distributed by the Saṅgha, one bhikkhu's portion was brought in another bhikkhu's bowl and set down nearby. The owner of the bowl, taking it to be his own, ate it. When the other found out, he reproached him: "You are no recluse!" He became remorseful… (and so on) … "There is no offence, bhikkhu, for one who perceives it as his own."

[148] Now at that time mango-thieves, having knocked down mangoes, took a bundle of them and went off. The owners pursued those thieves. The thieves, seeing the owners, dropped the bundle and fled. The bhikkhus, perceiving them as a rag from a dust-heap, had them formally offered and ate them. The owners reproached those bhikkhus: "You are no recluses!" They became remorseful… (and so on) … They reported this matter to the Blessed One. "With what mind did you act, bhikkhus?" "We perceived them as a rag from a dust-heap, Blessed One." "There is no offence, bhikkhus, for one who perceives it as a rag from a dust-heap." Now at that time rose-apple thieves… breadfruit thieves… jackfruit thieves… palmyra-fruit thieves… sugarcane thieves… timbaru-fruit thieves, having gathered timbaru fruits, took a bundle of them and went off. The owners pursued those thieves. The thieves, seeing the owners, dropped the bundle and fled. The bhikkhus, perceiving them as a rag from a dust-heap, had them formally offered and ate them. The owners reproached those bhikkhus: "You are no recluses!" They became remorseful… (and so on) … "There is no offence, bhikkhus, for one who perceives it as a rag from a dust-heap." Now at that time mango-thieves, having knocked down mangoes, took a bundle of them and went off. The owners pursued those thieves. The thieves, seeing the owners, dropped the bundle and fled. The bhikkhus, thinking "Let us eat before the owners see," ate them with thievish mind. The owners reproached those bhikkhus: "You are no recluses!" They became remorseful… (and so on) … "You, bhikkhus, have committed an offence entailing defeat." Now at that time rose-apple thieves… breadfruit thieves… jackfruit thieves… palmyra-fruit thieves… sugarcane thieves… timbaru-fruit thieves, having gathered timbaru fruits, took a bundle of them and went off. The owners pursued those thieves. The thieves, seeing the owners, dropped the bundle and fled. The bhikkhus, thinking "Let us eat before the owners see," ate them with thievish mind. The owners reproached those bhikkhus: "You are no recluses!" They became remorseful… (and so on) … "You, bhikkhus, have committed an offence entailing defeat." Now at that time a certain bhikkhu with thievish mind carried off a mango belonging to the Saṅgha… a rose-apple belonging to the Saṅgha… a breadfruit belonging to the Saṅgha… a jackfruit belonging to the Saṅgha… a palmyra fruit belonging to the Saṅgha… sugarcane belonging to the Saṅgha… a timbaru fruit belonging to the Saṅgha he carried off with thievish mind. He became remorseful… (and so on) … "You, bhikkhu, have committed an offence entailing defeat."

[149] Now at that time a certain bhikkhu, having gone to a flower garden, with thievish mind carried off already-picked flowers worth five māsakas. He became remorseful… (and so on) … "You, bhikkhu, have committed an offence entailing defeat." Now at that time a certain bhikkhu, having gone to a flower garden and picked flowers, with thievish mind carried off flowers worth five māsakas. He became remorseful… (and so on) … "You, bhikkhu, have committed an offence entailing defeat."

[150] Now at that time a certain bhikkhu who was going to a village said this to another bhikkhu: "Friend, bid me and I will speak to the family that supports you." He went, had a piece of cloth brought out, and used it himself. When the other found out, he reproached him: "You are no recluse!" He became remorseful… (and so on) … "There is no offence entailing defeat, bhikkhu. But, bhikkhus, one should not say 'Bid me and I will speak.' Whoever should say it commits an offence of wrong-doing." Now at that time a certain bhikkhu was going to a village. Another bhikkhu said this to that bhikkhu: "Friend, I bid you: speak to the family that supports me." He went, had a pair of cloths brought out, used one himself, and gave one to that bhikkhu. When the other found out, he reproached him: "You are no recluse!" He became remorseful… (and so on) … "There is no offence entailing defeat, bhikkhu. But, bhikkhus, one should not say 'I bid you: speak.' Whoever should say it commits an offence of wrong-doing." Now at that time a certain bhikkhu who was going to a village said this to another bhikkhu: "Friend, bid me and I will speak to the family that supports you." And that one too said: "I bid you: speak." He went, had an āḷhaka of ghee, a tulā of molasses and a doṇa of rice brought out, and used them himself. When the other found out, he reproached him: "You are no recluse!" He became remorseful… (and so on) … "There is no offence entailing defeat, bhikkhu. But, bhikkhus, one should not say 'Bid me and I will speak,' nor should one say 'I bid you: speak.' Whoever should say it commits an offence of wrong-doing."

[151] Now at that time a certain man who had taken a costly gem was travelling along the high road together with a certain bhikkhu. Then that man, seeing a toll-station, without the bhikkhu's knowledge put the gem into the bhikkhu's bag, and when they had passed the toll-station he took it back. The bhikkhu became remorseful… (and so on) … "With what mind did you act, bhikkhu?" "I did not know, Blessed One." "There is no offence, bhikkhu, for one who does not know." Now at that time a certain man who had taken a costly gem was travelling along the high road together with a certain bhikkhu. Then that man, seeing a toll-station, feigned illness and gave his bundle to that bhikkhu. Then, when they had passed the toll-station, that man said this to the bhikkhu: "Give me my bundle, venerable sir; I am not unwell." "But why, friend, did you act in such a way?" Then that man told the bhikkhu the matter. The bhikkhu became remorseful… (and so on) … "With what mind did you act, bhikkhu?" "I did not know, Blessed One." "There is no offence, bhikkhu, for one who does not know."
