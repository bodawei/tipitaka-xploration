Pārājikapāḷi — 1. Pārājikakaṇḍaṃ
vin01m.mul.xml, verses 172-179

[172] "Whoever": whoever, of whatever sort … and so on … "bhikkhu": … this is the one meant here by "bhikkhu". "Intentionally": knowing, recognising, deliberately, purposely committing the transgression. "A human being" means: from the time when the first mind arises, the first consciousness becomes manifest, in the mother's womb, up until the time of death — in that interval, that is called a human being. "Should deprive of life": he cuts off the life-faculty, breaks it off, destroys the continuity. "Or should seek out a knife-bringer for him": a sword, or a spear, or a javelin, or a club, or a stone, or a knife, or poison, or a rope. "Or should praise the beauty of death": he shows the danger in life, he speaks in praise of death. "Or should incite him to death": "Fetch a knife, or eat poison, or hang yourself with a rope and die." "Good man": this is a term of address. "What use to you is this evil, wretched life?" — an "evil" life means: compared with the life of the wealthy, the life of the poor is evil, base; compared with the life of the rich, the life of the propertyless is evil; compared with the life of the gods, the life of human beings is evil. A "wretched" life means: that of one whose hands are cut off, whose feet are cut off, whose hands and feet are cut off, whose ears are cut off, whose nose is cut off, whose ears and nose are cut off — "with this evil and this wretched life, death would be better for you than life." "So in mind and thought": whatever is mind, that is thought; whatever is thought, that is mind. "In intention of thought": perceiving death, intending death, aiming at death. "In many ways": by various methods. "Or should praise the beauty of death": he shows the danger in life, he speaks in praise of death: "Having died here, at the breaking up of the body after death you will arise in a good destination, a heavenly world; there, provided and furnished with the five divine strands of sensual pleasure, you will enjoy yourself." "Or should incite him to death": "Fetch a knife, or eat poison, or hang yourself with a rope and die, or fall into a pit, a chasm, or over a precipice." "This one too": this is said with reference to the preceding ones. "He becomes one entailing defeat": just as a broad stone slab, split in two, cannot be joined together again, so too a bhikkhu who intentionally deprives a human being of life is no recluse, no son of the Sakyan. Therefore it is said: "he becomes one entailing defeat." "Not in communion": communion means a single formal act, a single recitation, an equal training — this is called communion. He has none of that with them; therefore it is said "not in communion."

[173] By himself; by command; by a messenger; by a succession of messengers; by a messenger who goes astray; by a messenger who goes and returns; when not in private, perceiving it as private; when in private, perceiving it as not private; when not in private, perceiving it as not private; when in private, perceiving it as private; he praises by the body; he praises by speech; he praises by body and speech; he praises by a messenger; he praises by writing; a pit-trap; a leaning-support; placing a weapon nearby; medicine; presenting a form; presenting a sound; presenting a smell; presenting a taste; presenting a tangible; presenting a teaching; telling; instructing; making an appointed sign; making a signal.

[174] "By himself": he himself kills, with the body, or with something attached to the body, or with something thrown. "By command": having resolved upon it, he commands: "Stab thus, strike thus, kill thus." A bhikkhu commands a bhikkhu: "Deprive so-and-so of life" — an offence of wrong-doing. Taking him to be that one, he deprives that one of life — for both, an offence entailing defeat. A bhikkhu commands a bhikkhu: "Deprive so-and-so of life" — an offence of wrong-doing. Taking him to be that one, he deprives another of life — for the original instigator there is no offence; for the killer there is an offence entailing defeat. A bhikkhu commands a bhikkhu: "Deprive so-and-so of life" — an offence of wrong-doing. Taking him to be another, he deprives that one of life — for both, an offence entailing defeat. A bhikkhu commands a bhikkhu: "Deprive so-and-so of life" — an offence of wrong-doing. Taking him to be another, he deprives another of life — for the original instigator there is no offence; for the killer, an offence entailing defeat. A bhikkhu commands a bhikkhu: "Tell so-and-so: 'Let so-and-so tell so-and-so: let so-and-so deprive so-and-so of life'" — an offence of wrong-doing. He informs the next one — an offence of wrong-doing. The killer accepts — for the original instigator, a grave offence. He deprives that one of life — for all of them, an offence entailing defeat. A bhikkhu commands a bhikkhu: "Tell so-and-so: 'Let so-and-so tell so-and-so: let so-and-so deprive so-and-so of life'" — an offence of wrong-doing. He commands someone else — an offence of wrong-doing. The killer accepts — an offence of wrong-doing. He deprives that one of life — for the original instigator there is no offence; for the one who gave the command and for the killer, an offence entailing defeat. A bhikkhu commands a bhikkhu: "Deprive so-and-so of life" — an offence of wrong-doing. He goes and comes back again, saying: "I am not able to deprive him of life." The other commands him again: "When you are able, then deprive him of life" — an offence of wrong-doing. He deprives that one of life — for both, an offence entailing defeat. A bhikkhu commands a bhikkhu: "Deprive so-and-so of life" — an offence of wrong-doing. Having commanded, he becomes remorseful but does not let it be heard: "Do not kill him." The other deprives that one of life — for both, an offence entailing defeat. A bhikkhu commands a bhikkhu: "Deprive so-and-so of life" — an offence of wrong-doing. Having commanded, he becomes remorseful and lets it be heard: "Do not kill him." The other, saying "I was commanded by you," deprives that one of life — for the original instigator there is no offence; for the killer, an offence entailing defeat. A bhikkhu commands a bhikkhu: "Deprive so-and-so of life" — an offence of wrong-doing. Having commanded, he becomes remorseful and lets it be heard: "Do not kill him." The other says, "Very well," and desists — for both there is no offence.

[175] When not in private, perceiving it as private, he exclaims: "Oh, that so-and-so were slain!" — an offence of wrong-doing. When in private, perceiving it as not private, he exclaims: "Oh, that so-and-so were slain!" — an offence of wrong-doing. When not in private, perceiving it as not private, he exclaims: "Oh, that so-and-so were slain!" — an offence of wrong-doing. When in private, perceiving it as private, he exclaims: "Oh, that so-and-so were slain!" — an offence of wrong-doing. "He praises by the body" means: he makes a gesture with the body [signifying]: "Whoever dies thus gains wealth, or gains fame, or goes to heaven" — an offence of wrong-doing. Because of that praise, thinking "I shall die," the other produces painful feeling — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. "He praises by speech" means: he says with speech: "Whoever dies thus gains wealth, or gains fame, or goes to heaven" — an offence of wrong-doing. Because of that praise, thinking "I shall die," he produces painful feeling — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. "He praises by body and speech" means: he both makes a gesture with the body and says with speech: "Whoever dies thus gains wealth, or gains fame, or goes to heaven" — an offence of wrong-doing. Because of that praise, thinking "I shall die," he produces painful feeling — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. "He praises by a messenger" means: he gives a message to a messenger: "Whoever dies thus gains wealth, or gains fame, or goes to heaven" — an offence of wrong-doing. Having heard the messenger's message, thinking "I shall die," he produces painful feeling — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat.

[176] "He praises by writing" means: he inscribes writing: "Whoever dies thus gains wealth, or gains fame, or goes to heaven" — for each letter, an offence of wrong-doing. Having seen the writing, thinking "I shall die," he produces painful feeling — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. "A pit-trap" means: he digs a pit aiming at a [particular] person, thinking, "Having fallen in, he will die" — an offence of wrong-doing. When he has fallen in, painful feeling arises — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. He digs a pit without aiming at anyone, thinking, "Whoever falls in will die" — an offence of wrong-doing. A human being falls into it — an offence of wrong-doing. When he has fallen in, painful feeling arises — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. A yakkha, or a departed spirit, or an animal in human form falls into it — an offence of wrong-doing. When it has fallen in, painful feeling arises — an offence of wrong-doing. It dies — a grave offence. An animal falls into it — an offence of wrong-doing. When it has fallen in, painful feeling arises — an offence of wrong-doing. It dies — an offence of expiation.

[177] "A leaning-support" means: on a leaning-support he sets a knife, or smears it with poison, or makes it weak, or places it above a pit, a chasm, or a precipice, thinking, "Having fallen, he will die" — an offence of wrong-doing. Through the knife, or the poison, or the fall, painful feeling arises — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. "Placing a weapon nearby" means: he places nearby a sword, or a spear, or a javelin, or a club, or a stone, or a knife, or poison, or a rope, thinking, "By this he will die" — an offence of wrong-doing. Thinking "I shall die by that," the other produces painful feeling — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. "Medicine" means: he gives ghee, or fresh butter, or oil, or honey, or molasses, thinking, "Having tasted this, he will die" — an offence of wrong-doing. When he has tasted it, painful feeling arises — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat.

[178] "Presenting a form" means: he brings up a disagreeable form, frightful and terrifying, thinking, "Having seen this, he will be terrified and will die" — an offence of wrong-doing. Having seen it, he is terrified — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. He brings up an agreeable form, thinking, "Having seen this, he will pine away from not obtaining it and will die" — an offence of wrong-doing. Having seen it, he pines away from not obtaining it — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. "Presenting a sound" means: he brings up a disagreeable sound, frightful and terrifying, thinking, "Having heard this, he will be terrified and will die" — an offence of wrong-doing. Having heard it, he is terrified — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. He brings up an agreeable sound, lovely and heart-stirring, thinking, "Having heard this, he will pine away from not obtaining it and will die" — an offence of wrong-doing. Having heard it, he pines away from not obtaining it — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. "Presenting a smell" means: he brings up a disagreeable smell, disgusting and repulsive, thinking, "Having smelt this, he will die through disgust and revulsion" — an offence of wrong-doing. When he has smelt it, painful feeling arises through disgust and revulsion — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. He brings up an agreeable smell, thinking, "Having smelt this, he will pine away from not obtaining it and will die" — an offence of wrong-doing. Having smelt it, he pines away from not obtaining it — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. "Presenting a taste" means: he brings up a disagreeable taste, disgusting and repulsive, thinking, "Having tasted this, he will die through disgust and revulsion" — an offence of wrong-doing. When he has tasted it, painful feeling arises through disgust and revulsion — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. He brings up an agreeable taste, thinking, "Having tasted this, he will pine away from not obtaining it and will die" — an offence of wrong-doing. Having tasted it, he pines away from not obtaining it — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. "Presenting a tangible" means: he brings up a disagreeable tangible, painful to touch and rough to touch, thinking, "Touched by this, he will die" — an offence of wrong-doing. When he is touched by it, painful feeling arises — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. He brings up an agreeable tangible, pleasant to touch and soft to touch, thinking, "Touched by this, he will pine away from not obtaining it and will die" — an offence of wrong-doing. Touched by it, he pines away from not obtaining it — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. "Presenting a teaching" means: to one bound for hell he speaks talk of hell, thinking, "Having heard this, he will be terrified and will die" — an offence of wrong-doing. Having heard it, he is terrified — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. To one of good deeds he speaks talk of heaven, thinking, "Having heard this, he will be intent upon it and will die" — an offence of wrong-doing. Having heard it, being intent upon it and thinking "I shall die," he produces painful feeling — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat.

[179] "Telling" means: being asked, he says: "Die in this way. Whoever dies thus gains wealth, or gains fame, or goes to heaven" — an offence of wrong-doing. Because of that telling, thinking "I shall die," he produces painful feeling — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. "Instructing" means: unasked, he says: "Die in this way. Whoever dies thus gains wealth, or gains fame, or goes to heaven" — an offence of wrong-doing. Because of that instruction, thinking "I shall die," he produces painful feeling — a grave offence. He dies — an offence entailing defeat. "Making an appointed sign" means: he makes an appointment for before the meal, or after the meal, or by night, or by day: "At that appointed time deprive him of life" — an offence of wrong-doing. At that appointed time he deprives him of life — for both, an offence entailing defeat. If he deprives him of life before or after that appointed time — for the original instigator there is no offence; for the killer, an offence entailing defeat. "Making a signal" means: he makes a signal: "I shall wink my eye, or raise my eyebrow, or lift my head; at that signal deprive him of life" — an offence of wrong-doing. At that signal he deprives him of life — for both, an offence entailing defeat. If he deprives him of life before or after that signal — for the original instigator there is no offence; for the killer, an offence entailing defeat. There is no offence if it is unintentional, if he does not know, if he is not aiming at death, if he is mad, if he is the first offender.
