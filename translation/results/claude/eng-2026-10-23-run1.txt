Pārājikapāḷi — 1. Pārājikakaṇḍaṃ
vin01m.mul.xml, verses 162-171

[162] At that time the Buddha, the Blessed One, was dwelling at Vesālī in the Great Wood, in the hall with the peaked roof. Now at that time the Blessed One was giving the bhikkhus a talk on the unattractive in many ways, was speaking in praise of the unattractive, was speaking in praise of the development of the perception of the unattractive, was speaking, with reference to one case after another, in praise of the attainment based on the unattractive. Then the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus: "Bhikkhus, I wish to go into solitary retreat for half a month. No one is to approach me except the one who brings me almsfood." "Yes, venerable sir," those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One, and no one approached the Blessed One except the one who brought him almsfood. The bhikkhus thought: "The Blessed One has given a talk on the unattractive in many ways, has spoken in praise of the unattractive, has spoken in praise of the development of the perception of the unattractive, has spoken, with reference to one case after another, in praise of the attainment based on the unattractive"; and they dwelt applying themselves to the practice of the development of the perception of the unattractive in its many aspects and modes. They became troubled by their own bodies, ashamed of them, disgusted with them. Just as a woman or a man, young, youthful, fond of adornment, with head freshly washed, would be troubled, ashamed and disgusted if the carcass of a snake or the carcass of a dog or the carcass of a human being were hung around their neck, just so those bhikkhus, being troubled by their own bodies, ashamed of them, disgusted with them, deprived themselves of life, and deprived one another of life, and going up to Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse they said: "Please, friend, deprive us of life; this bowl and robe will be yours." Then Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse, hired with bowls and robes, deprived a number of bhikkhus of life and, taking his blood-stained sword, went to the river Vaggamudā.

[163] Then, as Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse was washing that blood-stained sword, remorse and regret arose in him: "It is indeed a loss for me, not a gain; it is indeed ill-gotten by me, not well-gotten. Much demerit has indeed been produced by me, in that I have deprived of life bhikkhus who were virtuous and of good character." Then a certain deity of Māra's company, coming over the water without breaking its surface, said this to Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse: "Good, good, worthy man! It is a gain for you, worthy man; it is well-gotten by you, worthy man. Much merit has been produced by you, worthy man, in that you have ferried across those who had not crossed over." Then Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse thought: "It is indeed a gain for me, it is indeed well-gotten by me, much merit has indeed been produced by me, I ferry across those who have not crossed over!" and taking a sharp sword he went from dwelling to dwelling, from cell to cell, saying: "Who has not crossed over? Whom shall I ferry across?" Thereupon, among those bhikkhus who were not free from lust, at that time there arose fear, there arose terror, there arose horripilation. But among those bhikkhus who were free from lust, at that time there arose no fear, no terror, no horripilation. Then Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse deprived one bhikkhu of life in a single day, and he deprived two bhikkhus of life in a single day, and three bhikkhus of life in a single day, and four bhikkhus of life in a single day, and five bhikkhus of life in a single day, and ten bhikkhus of life in a single day, and twenty bhikkhus of life in a single day, and thirty bhikkhus of life in a single day, and forty bhikkhus of life in a single day, and fifty bhikkhus of life in a single day, and sixty bhikkhus of life in a single day.

[164] Then the Blessed One, at the end of that half-month, emerged from his solitary retreat and addressed the venerable Ānanda: "How is it, Ānanda, that the Saṅgha of bhikkhus seems to have grown so thin?" "It is because, venerable sir, the Blessed One gave the bhikkhus a talk on the unattractive in many ways, spoke in praise of the unattractive, spoke in praise of the development of the perception of the unattractive, spoke, with reference to one case after another, in praise of the attainment based on the unattractive. And those bhikkhus, venerable sir, thinking, 'The Blessed One has given a talk on the unattractive in many ways, has spoken in praise of the unattractive, has spoken in praise of the development of the perception of the unattractive, has spoken, with reference to one case after another, in praise of the attainment based on the unattractive,' dwelt applying themselves to the practice of the development of the perception of the unattractive in its many aspects and modes. They became troubled by their own bodies, ashamed of them, disgusted with them. Just as a woman or a man, young, youthful, fond of adornment, with head freshly washed, would be troubled, ashamed and disgusted if the carcass of a snake or the carcass of a dog or the carcass of a human being were hung around their neck, just so those bhikkhus, being troubled by their own bodies, ashamed of them, disgusted with them, deprived themselves of life, and deprived one another of life, and going up to Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse they said: 'Please, friend, deprive us of life; this bowl and robe will be yours.' Then, venerable sir, Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse, hired with bowls and robes, deprived one bhikkhu of life in a single day … and he deprived sixty bhikkhus of life in a single day. It would be good, venerable sir, if the Blessed One would explain some other method by which this Saṅgha of bhikkhus might become established in final knowledge." "Well then, Ānanda, assemble in the attendance hall all the bhikkhus who live in dependence on Vesālī." "Yes, venerable sir," the venerable Ānanda replied to the Blessed One, and having assembled in the attendance hall all the bhikkhus who lived in dependence on Vesālī, he approached the Blessed One; having approached, he said this to the Blessed One: "The Saṅgha of bhikkhus is assembled, venerable sir; let the Blessed One now do as he thinks fit."

[165] Then the Blessed One went to the attendance hall; having gone there, he sat down on the seat prepared. Seated, the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus: "This concentration through mindfulness of breathing too, bhikkhus, when developed and frequently practised, is peaceful and sublime, unadulterated and a pleasant abiding, and it makes evil unwholesome states vanish and subside on the spot whenever they arise. Just as, bhikkhus, in the last month of the hot season, when dust and dirt have been stirred up, a great unseasonal rain-cloud makes them vanish and subside on the spot, just so, bhikkhus, concentration through mindfulness of breathing, when developed and frequently practised, is peaceful and sublime, unadulterated and a pleasant abiding, and it makes evil unwholesome states vanish and subside on the spot whenever they arise. And how, bhikkhus, is concentration through mindfulness of breathing developed, how is it frequently practised, so that it is peaceful and sublime, unadulterated and a pleasant abiding, and makes evil unwholesome states vanish and subside on the spot whenever they arise? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu, gone to the forest or to the root of a tree or to an empty hut, sits down, folding his legs crosswise, setting his body erect, and establishing mindfulness before him. Just mindful he breathes in, mindful he breathes out. Breathing in long, he knows, 'I breathe in long'; breathing out long, he knows, 'I breathe out long.' Breathing in short, he knows, 'I breathe in short'; breathing out short, he knows, 'I breathe out short.' He trains thus: 'Experiencing the whole body, I shall breathe in'; he trains thus: 'Experiencing the whole body, I shall breathe out.' He trains thus: 'Calming the bodily formation, I shall breathe in'; he trains thus: 'Calming the bodily formation, I shall breathe out.' He trains thus: 'Experiencing rapture, I shall breathe in'; he trains thus: 'Experiencing rapture, I shall breathe out.' He trains thus: 'Experiencing pleasure, I shall breathe in'; he trains thus: 'Experiencing pleasure, I shall breathe out.' He trains thus: 'Experiencing the mental formation, I shall breathe in'; he trains thus: 'Experiencing the mental formation, I shall breathe out.' He trains thus: 'Calming the mental formation, I shall breathe in'; he trains thus: 'Calming the mental formation, I shall breathe out.' He trains thus: 'Experiencing the mind, I shall breathe in'; he trains thus: 'Experiencing the mind, I shall breathe out.' 'Gladdening the mind …' … 'Concentrating the mind …' … 'Liberating the mind …' … 'Contemplating impermanence …' … 'Contemplating fading away …' … 'Contemplating cessation …' … he trains thus: 'Contemplating relinquishment, I shall breathe in'; he trains thus: 'Contemplating relinquishment, I shall breathe out.' Thus developed, bhikkhus, thus frequently practised, concentration through mindfulness of breathing is peaceful and sublime, unadulterated and a pleasant abiding, and it makes evil unwholesome states vanish and subside on the spot whenever they arise."

[166] Then the Blessed One, on that occasion and in that connection, had the Saṅgha of bhikkhus assembled and questioned the bhikkhus: "Is it true, bhikkhus, that bhikkhus deprived themselves of life, and deprived one another of life, and going up to Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse said: 'Please, friend, deprive us of life; this bowl and robe will be yours'?" "It is true, Blessed One." The Buddha, the Blessed One, rebuked them: "It is unbecoming, bhikkhus, of those bhikkhus, unsuitable, improper, unworthy of a recluse, not allowable, not to be done. How could those bhikkhus, bhikkhus, deprive themselves of life, and deprive one another of life, and go up to Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse and say: 'Please, friend, deprive us of life; this bowl and robe will be yours'? This, bhikkhus, is not for the confidence of those without confidence … And thus, bhikkhus, you should recite this training rule:

[167] "Whatever bhikkhu should intentionally deprive a human being of life, or should look about for a knife-bringer for him, he too is one who is defeated, not in communion." Thus was this training rule laid down by the Blessed One for the bhikkhus.

[168] Now at that time a certain lay follower was ill. His wife was beautiful, good to look at, charming. The bhikkhus of the group of six became infatuated with that woman. Then it occurred to the bhikkhus of the group of six: "If that lay follower lives, friends, we shall not get that woman. Come, friends, let us praise the beauty of death to that lay follower." Then the bhikkhus of the group of six went to that lay follower; having gone, they said this to that lay follower: "You, lay follower, have done what is good, have done what is wholesome, have made yourself a shelter from fear; you have done no evil, done nothing cruel, done nothing wicked. Good has been done by you, evil has not been done by you. What use to you is this evil, miserable life? Death would be better for you than life. When you have passed away from here, with the breakup of the body, after death, you will be reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world. There, endowed and furnished with the five strands of divine sensual pleasure, you will enjoy yourself."

[169] Then that lay follower thought: "What the masters say is true. For I have done what is good, done what is wholesome, made myself a shelter from fear; I have done no evil, done nothing cruel, done nothing wicked. Good has been done by me, evil has not been done by me. What use to me is this evil, miserable life? Death would be better for me than life. When I have passed away from here, with the breakup of the body, after death, I shall be reborn in a good destination, in a heavenly world. There, endowed and furnished with the five strands of divine sensual pleasure, I shall enjoy myself." So he ate unsuitable foods, chewed unsuitable foods to chew, tasted unsuitable things to taste, and drank unsuitable drinks. As he ate unsuitable foods, chewed unsuitable foods to chew, tasted unsuitable things to taste, and drank unsuitable drinks, a severe illness arose in him. From that very illness he died. His wife grumbled, complained and spread it about: "These recluses, sons of the Sakyans, are shameless, immoral, liars! And yet these will claim to be practisers of Dhamma, practisers of what is right, livers of the holy life, speakers of truth, virtuous, of good character! There is no recluseship in them, there is no brahminhood in them; their recluseship is destroyed, their brahminhood is destroyed; where is their recluseship, where is their brahminhood? They are devoid of recluseship, they are devoid of brahminhood. These praised the beauty of death to my husband; by these my husband has been killed." Other people too grumbled, complained and spread it about: "These recluses, sons of the Sakyans, are shameless, immoral, liars! And yet these will claim to be practisers of Dhamma, practisers of what is right, livers of the holy life, speakers of truth, virtuous, of good character! There is no recluseship in them, there is no brahminhood in them; their recluseship is destroyed, their brahminhood is destroyed; where is their recluseship, where is their brahminhood? They are devoid of recluseship, they are devoid of brahminhood. These praised the beauty of death to the lay follower; by these the lay follower has been killed." The bhikkhus heard those people grumbling, complaining and spreading it about. Those bhikkhus who were of few wishes … grumbled, complained and spread it about: "How could the bhikkhus of the group of six praise the beauty of death to a lay follower!"

[170] Then those bhikkhus, having rebuked the bhikkhus of the group of six in many ways, reported the matter to the Blessed One … "Is it true, bhikkhus, that you praised the beauty of death to a lay follower?" "It is true, Blessed One." The Buddha, the Blessed One, rebuked them: "It is unbecoming, foolish men, unsuitable, improper, unworthy of a recluse, not allowable, not to be done. How could you, foolish men, praise the beauty of death to a lay follower! This, foolish men, is not for the confidence of those without confidence … And thus, bhikkhus, you should recite this training rule:

[171] "Whatever bhikkhu should intentionally deprive a human being of life, or should look about for a knife-bringer for him, or should praise the beauty of death, or should incite him to death, saying, 'Hey there, man, what use to you is this evil, miserable life? Death would be better for you than life,' — if, with such a mind and intention, with such a thought and purpose, he should in various ways praise the beauty of death or incite him to death, he too is one who is defeated, not in communion."
