Pārājikapāḷi — 1. Pārājikakaṇḍaṃ

[193] At that time the Buddha, the Blessed One, was dwelling at Vesālī in the Great Wood, in the hall with the peaked roof. Now at that time a number of bhikkhus who were acquaintances and intimate friends entered upon the rains residence on the bank of the river Vaggumudā. At that time there was a famine among the Vajjīs, a time of scarcity, with the crops blighted and turned to straw, with food distributed by ticket, and it was not easy to keep oneself going by gleaning and begging. Then it occurred to those bhikkhus: "At present there is a famine among the Vajjīs, a time of scarcity, the crops blighted and turned to straw, food distributed by ticket, and it is not easy to keep oneself going by gleaning and begging. By what means might we live out the rains in harmony, on friendly terms, without quarrelling, and at ease, and not be troubled for almsfood?" Some spoke thus: "Come, friends, let us undertake work for the householders; then they will think to give to us. Thus shall we live out the rains in harmony, on friendly terms, without quarrelling, and at ease, and shall not be troubled for almsfood." Some spoke thus: "Enough, friends, why undertake work for the householders! Come, friends, let us carry messages for the householders; then they will think to give to us. Thus shall we live out the rains in harmony, on friendly terms, without quarrelling, and at ease, and shall not be troubled for almsfood." Some spoke thus: "Enough, friends, why undertake work for the householders! Why carry messages for the householders! Come, friends, let us speak to the householders in praise of one another's superhuman states: 'Such and such a bhikkhu is one who has gained the first jhāna, such and such a bhikkhu is one who has gained the second jhāna, such and such a bhikkhu is one who has gained the third jhāna, such and such a bhikkhu is one who has gained the fourth jhāna, such and such a bhikkhu is a stream-enterer, such and such a bhikkhu is a once-returner, such and such a bhikkhu is a non-returner, such and such a bhikkhu is an arahant, such and such a bhikkhu is one of the three true knowledges, such and such a bhikkhu is one of the six direct knowledges.' Then they will think to give to us. Thus shall we live out the rains in harmony, on friendly terms, without quarrelling, and at ease, and shall not be troubled for almsfood." "This indeed, friends, is the better course, that we should speak to the householders in praise of one another's superhuman states."

[194] Then those bhikkhus spoke to the householders in praise of one another's superhuman states: "Such and such a bhikkhu is one who has gained the first jhāna… such and such a bhikkhu is one of the six direct knowledges." Then those people thought: "It is truly a gain for us, truly well gained by us, that such bhikkhus as these have entered upon the rains with us; never before have such bhikkhus as these entered upon the rains with us as these bhikkhus who are virtuous and of lovely character." And they did not themselves consume such food, nor give such food to their mothers and fathers, nor to their wives and children, nor to their slaves, workers and servants, nor to their friends and colleagues, nor to their kinsmen and blood-relations, as they gave to the bhikkhus. They did not themselves eat, taste and drink such foods, delicacies and drinks, nor give them to their mothers and fathers, nor to their wives and children, nor to their slaves, workers and servants, nor to their friends and colleagues, nor to their kinsmen and blood-relations, as they gave to the bhikkhus. Then those bhikkhus became handsome, with plump faculties, with clear complexions and bright skin. Now it is the custom for bhikkhus who have completed the rains to go to see the Blessed One. So those bhikkhus, having completed the rains, at the end of the three months set their lodgings in order, took bowl and robe, and set out for Vesālī. In due course they came to Vesālī, the Great Wood, the hall with the peaked roof, to the Blessed One; and having approached, they paid homage to the Blessed One and sat down to one side. Now at that time the bhikkhus who had completed the rains in the various districts were lean, rough, of poor complexion, grown very sallow, their limbs all veins; but the bhikkhus from the bank of the Vaggumudā were handsome, with plump faculties, with clear complexions and bright skin. Now it is the custom for the Buddhas, the Blessed Ones, to exchange friendly greetings with incoming bhikkhus. So the Blessed One said to the bhikkhus from the bank of the Vaggumudā: "I hope, bhikkhus, that you are bearing up, that you are keeping going; I hope you lived out the rains in harmony, on friendly terms, without quarrelling, and at ease, and were not troubled for almsfood?" "We are bearing up, Blessed One, we are keeping going, Blessed One. And we lived out the rains, venerable sir, in harmony, on friendly terms, without quarrelling, and at ease, and were not troubled for almsfood." Tathāgatas sometimes ask knowing, and knowing sometimes do not ask… For two reasons the Buddhas, the Blessed Ones, question the bhikkhus: "We shall teach the Dhamma, or we shall lay down a training rule for the disciples." Then the Blessed One said to the bhikkhus from the bank of the Vaggumudā: "But in what way, bhikkhus, did you live out the rains in harmony, on friendly terms, without quarrelling, and at ease, and were not troubled for almsfood?" Then those bhikkhus reported the matter to the Blessed One. "But was it true of you, bhikkhus?" "It was not true, Blessed One." The Buddha, the Blessed One, rebuked them: "It is unseemly, foolish men, unsuitable, improper, unworthy of a recluse, not allowable, not to be done. How could you, foolish men, for the sake of your bellies, speak to the householders in praise of one another's superhuman states! Better for you, foolish men, that your bellies were cut open with a sharp butcher's knife than that for the sake of your bellies you should speak to householders in praise of one another's superhuman states! Why is that? For from that cause, foolish men, you would incur death or deadly suffering, but you would not on that account, at the breaking up of the body after death, arise in a state of deprivation, a bad destination, the abyss, hell. But from this cause, foolish men, you would at the breaking up of the body after death arise in a state of deprivation, a bad destination, the abyss, hell. This, foolish men, is not for the confidence of those without confidence…" and having rebuked them and given a talk on the Dhamma, he addressed the bhikkhus:

[195] "There are, bhikkhus, these five great thieves existing and found in the world. What five? Here, bhikkhus, it occurs to a certain great thief: 'When shall I, surrounded by a hundred or by a thousand, roam about in villages, towns and royal capitals, slaying and causing to be slain, cutting and causing to be cut, burning and causing to be burnt!' And at a later time, surrounded by a hundred or by a thousand, he roams about in villages, towns and royal capitals, slaying and causing to be slain, cutting and causing to be cut, burning and causing to be burnt. Just so, bhikkhus, it occurs to a certain evil bhikkhu here: 'When shall I, surrounded by a hundred or by a thousand, wander on tour in villages, towns and royal capitals, honoured, respected, revered, venerated and esteemed by householders and those gone forth, a gainer of robes, almsfood, lodgings and medicinal requisites for the sick!' And at a later time, surrounded by a hundred or by a thousand, he wanders on tour in villages, towns and royal capitals, honoured, respected, revered, venerated and esteemed by householders and those gone forth, a gainer of robes, almsfood, lodgings and medicinal requisites for the sick. This, bhikkhus, is the first great thief existing and found in the world. "Again, bhikkhus, a certain evil bhikkhu here, having mastered the Dhamma and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata, passes it off as his own. This, bhikkhus, is the second great thief existing and found in the world. "Again, bhikkhus, a certain evil bhikkhu here charges a pure person, one leading the holy life in perfect purity, with a groundless accusation of unchastity. This, bhikkhus, is the third great thief existing and found in the world. "Again, bhikkhus, a certain evil bhikkhu here, with whatever heavy property and heavy requisites belong to the Saṅgha — that is to say, a park, the site of a park, a dwelling, the site of a dwelling, a bed, a bench, a mattress, a pillow, a metal pot, a metal jar, a metal vessel, a metal basin, a chopper, an axe, a hatchet, a hoe, a spade, creepers, bamboo, muñja grass, reed grass, grass, clay, wooden goods, earthenware goods — with these he ingratiates himself with and wheedles householders. This, bhikkhus, is the fourth great thief existing and found in the world. "In this world with its devas, with its Māras, with its Brahmās, in this generation with its recluses and brahmins, its devas and humans, this is the chief great thief: he who boasts of a superhuman state that is non-existent and untrue. Why is that? You have eaten the country's almsfood by way of theft, bhikkhus." Then the Blessed One, having rebuked those bhikkhus from the bank of the Vaggumudā in many ways, spoke of being difficult to support, difficult to maintain… "And thus, bhikkhus, you should recite this training rule: "Whatever bhikkhu, without direct knowledge, should claim a superhuman state of himself, knowledge and vision worthy of the noble ones, saying 'Thus I know, thus I see,' and then at a later time, whether being cross-questioned or not being cross-questioned, having fallen into an offence and looking for purification, should say thus: 'Not knowing, friends, I said I know; not seeing, I said I see. What I said was empty and false' — he too incurs an offence entailing defeat, he is not in communion." Thus this training rule was laid down by the Blessed One for the bhikkhus.

[196] Now at that time a number of bhikkhus, perceiving as seen what was not seen, as attained what was not attained, as reached what was not reached, as realised what was not realised, declared final knowledge through overestimation. Afterwards their minds inclined to lust, their minds inclined to hatred, their minds inclined to delusion. They felt remorse: "A training rule has been laid down by the Blessed One. And we, perceiving as seen what was not seen, as attained what was not attained, as reached what was not reached, as realised what was not realised, declared final knowledge through overestimation. Can it be that we have fallen into an offence entailing defeat?" They reported the matter to the venerable Ānanda. The venerable Ānanda reported the matter to the Blessed One. "There are, Ānanda, those bhikkhus who, perceiving as seen what was not seen, as attained what was not attained, as reached what was not reached, as realised what was not realised, declare final knowledge through overestimation. But that is not to be reckoned as an offence." "And thus, bhikkhus, you should recite this training rule:

[197] "Whatever bhikkhu, without direct knowledge, should claim a superhuman state of himself, knowledge and vision worthy of the noble ones, saying 'Thus I know, thus I see,' and then at a later time, whether being cross-questioned or not being cross-questioned, having fallen into an offence and looking for purification, should say thus: 'Not knowing, friends, I said I know; not seeing, I said I see. What I said was empty and false' — except through overestimation, he too incurs an offence entailing defeat, he is not in communion."

[198] Whatever means: whoever, of whatever sort… bhikkhu means:… this is the one intended by "bhikkhu" in this sense. Without direct knowledge means: not knowing, not seeing in himself a wholesome state that is non-existent, untrue, not found, thinking "There is in me a wholesome state." Superhuman state means: jhāna, liberation, concentration, attainment, knowledge and vision, development of the path, realisation of the fruits, abandoning of the defilements, the mind's being unobstructed, delight in an empty hut. Of himself means: he applies those wholesome states to himself, or he applies himself to those wholesome states. Knowledge means: the three true knowledges. Vision means: that which is knowledge is vision, that which is vision is knowledge. Should claim means: should announce it to a woman or a man, to a householder or to one gone forth. Thus I know, thus I see means: "I know these states, I see these states, and these states are in me, and I am seen in these states." Then at a later time means: when the moment, the instant, the second in which the claim was made has passed. Being cross-questioned means: being cross-questioned about the matter he has admitted: "What have you attained? How have you attained it? When did you attain it? Where did you attain it? Which defilements have you abandoned? Which states have you gained?" Not being cross-questioned means: not being spoken to by anyone. Having fallen into an offence means: being of evil desires, overcome by desire, having boasted of a superhuman state that is non-existent and untrue, he has fallen into an offence entailing defeat. Looking for purification means: wishing to become a householder, or wishing to become a lay follower, or wishing to become a monastery attendant, or wishing to become a novice. "Not knowing, friends, I said I know; not seeing, I said I see" means: "I do not know these states, I do not see these states, these states are not in me, and I am not seen in these states." "What I said was empty and false" means: "What I spoke was empty, what I spoke was false, what I spoke was untrue, I spoke not knowing." Except through overestimation means: setting aside overestimation. He too: this is said with reference to the previous ones. Incurs an offence entailing defeat means: just as a palm tree with its crown cut off is incapable of growing again, so a bhikkhu who, being of evil desires, overcome by desire, has boasted of a superhuman state that is non-existent and untrue, is not a recluse, not a son of the Sakyan. Therefore it is said, "he incurs an offence entailing defeat." Not in communion means: communion is a single formal act, a single recitation, an equal training — this is called communion. He has none of that with them. Therefore it is said, "not in communion."

[199] Superhuman state means: jhāna, liberation, concentration, attainment, knowledge and vision, development of the path, realisation of the fruits, abandoning of the defilements, the mind's being unobstructed, delight in an empty hut. Jhāna means: the first jhāna, the second jhāna, the third jhāna, the fourth jhāna. Liberation means: the empty liberation, the signless liberation, the undirected liberation. Concentration means: the empty concentration, the signless concentration, the undirected concentration. Attainment means: the empty attainment, the signless attainment, the undirected attainment. Knowledge and vision means: the three true knowledges. Development of the path means: the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right strivings, the four bases of psychic power, the five faculties, the five powers, the seven factors of enlightenment, the noble eightfold path. Realisation of the fruits means: the realisation of the fruit of stream-entry, the realisation of the fruit of once-returning, the realisation of the fruit of non-returning, the realisation of arahantship. Abandoning of the defilements means: the abandoning of lust, the abandoning of hatred, the abandoning of delusion. The mind's being unobstructed means: the mind's being unobstructed by lust, the mind's being unobstructed by hatred, the mind's being unobstructed by delusion. Delight in an empty hut means: delight in an empty hut through the first jhāna, delight in an empty hut through the second jhāna, delight in an empty hut through the third jhāna, delight in an empty hut through the fourth jhāna.
