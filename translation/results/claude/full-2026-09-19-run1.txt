[87] Then Vassakāra the brahmin, chief minister of Magadha, while inspecting the works in Rājagaha, approached the overseer of the timber-yard and said to him: "I say, those logs belonging to the king's store, set aside for the repair of the city in case of emergency — where are those logs?" "Sire, those logs have been given by His Majesty to master Dhaniya the potter's son." Then Vassakāra the brahmin, chief minister of Magadha, was displeased: "How can His Majesty give to Dhaniya the potter's son the logs belonging to the king's store, set aside for the repair of the city in case of emergency!" Then Vassakāra the brahmin, chief minister of Magadha, approached King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha and said to him: "Is it true, sire, that Your Majesty gave to Dhaniya the potter's son the logs belonging to the king's store, set aside for the repair of the city in case of emergency?" "Who says so?" "The overseer of the timber-yard, sire." "Then, brahmin, have the overseer of the timber-yard arrested." So Vassakāra the brahmin, chief minister of Magadha, ordered the overseer of the timber-yard to be bound. The venerable Dhaniya the potter's son saw the overseer of the timber-yard being led away in bonds. Seeing him, he said to the overseer of the timber-yard: "Friend, why are you being led away in bonds?" "Because of that business of the logs, venerable sir." "Go, friend, I too am coming." "Come, venerable sir, before I am executed."

[88] Then the venerable Dhaniya the potter's son approached the residence of King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha and sat down on the seat made ready. Then King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha approached the venerable Dhaniya the potter's son, paid homage to him, and sat down to one side. Seated to one side, King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha said to the venerable Dhaniya the potter's son: "Is it true, venerable sir, that I gave to the master the logs belonging to the king's store, set aside for the repair of the city in case of emergency?" "Yes, great king." "Venerable sir, we kings have much business and many duties; even having given something, we may not remember. Please, venerable sir, remind me." "Do you remember, great king, that when you were newly consecrated you spoke these words: 'Let recluses and brahmins make use of grass, wood and water — it is already given'?" "I remember, venerable sir. There are recluses and brahmins who are conscientious, scrupulous, desirous of training; remorse arises in them even over a trifle. It was with reference to them that I spoke, and moreover I meant what is in the forest and unowned. And you, venerable sir, think under that pretext to carry off logs that were not given! How could one like me strike or bind or banish a recluse or a brahmin living in my realm! Go, venerable sir, you are let off on account of your hair. Do not do such a thing again." People looked down upon it, criticized it, and spread it about: "These recluses, sons of the Sakyans, are shameless, immoral, liars. Yet they will claim to be walkers in the Dhamma, walkers in peace, walkers in the holy life, speakers of truth, virtuous, of good character! There is no recluseship in them, there is no brahminhood in them. Their recluseship is lost, their brahminhood is lost. How can there be recluseship in them, how can there be brahminhood in them! They are bereft of recluseship, they are bereft of brahminhood. They even cheat the king — what then of other people!" Bhikkhus heard those people looking down upon it, criticizing it and spreading it about. Those bhikkhus who were of few wishes, content, conscientious, scrupulous, desirous of training, looked down upon it, criticized it and spread it about: "How can the venerable Dhaniya the potter's son take what was not given, belonging to the king!" Then those bhikkhus, having rebuked the venerable Dhaniya the potter's son in many ways, reported the matter to the Blessed One. Then the Blessed One, on that occasion, in that connection, had the Saṅgha of bhikkhus assembled and questioned the venerable Dhaniya the potter's son: "Is it true, Dhaniya, that you took what was not given, belonging to the king?" "It is true, Blessed One." The Buddha, the Blessed One, rebuked him: "It is unbecoming, foolish man, unsuitable, improper, unworthy of a recluse, not allowable, not to be done. How can you, foolish man, take what was not given, belonging to the king! This, foolish man, is not for the confidence of those without confidence, nor for the increase of those with confidence; rather, foolish man, it is for the lack of confidence in those without confidence and for wavering in some of those with confidence." Now at that time a certain former judicial officer, a chief minister, had gone forth among the bhikkhus and was sitting not far from the Blessed One. Then the Blessed One said to that bhikkhu: "For how much, bhikkhu, does King Seniya Bimbisāra of Magadha, having seized a thief, have him executed or bound or banished?" "For a pāda, Blessed One, or for the worth of a pāda." Now at that time in Rājagaha a pāda was five māsakas. Then the Blessed One, having rebuked the venerable Dhaniya the potter's son in many ways, having spoken of being difficult to support... and so on... "And thus, bhikkhus, you should recite this training rule:

[89] "Whatever bhikkhu should take, by way of theft, what is not given — such a taking of what is not given as when kings, having seized a thief, would execute him or bind him or banish him, saying 'You are a thief, you are a fool, you are deluded, you are a robber' — a bhikkhu taking in such a way what is not given is also one who has incurred an offence entailing defeat, and is not in communion." Thus this training rule was laid down by the Blessed One for the bhikkhus.

[90] Now at that time the bhikkhus of the group of six went to the dyers' drying-ground, stole a dyers' bundle, brought it to the monastery and divided it up. The bhikkhus said: "You are of great merit, friends. Much robe-material has come to you." "How should we have merit, friends? Just now we went to the dyers' drying-ground and stole a dyers' bundle." "But, friends, has not a training rule been laid down by the Blessed One? Why did you steal a dyers' bundle, friends?" "True, friends, a training rule has been laid down by the Blessed One; but that concerns a village, not the forest." "But, friends, is it not just the same? It is unbecoming, friends, unsuitable, improper, unworthy of a recluse, not allowable, not to be done. How can you, friends, steal a dyers' bundle! This, friends, is not for the confidence of those without confidence, nor for the increase of those with confidence; rather, friends, it is for the lack of confidence in those without confidence and for wavering in some of those with confidence." Then those bhikkhus, having rebuked the bhikkhus of the group of six in many ways, reported the matter to the Blessed One. Then the Blessed One, on that occasion, in that connection, had the Saṅgha of bhikkhus assembled and questioned the bhikkhus of the group of six: "Is it true, bhikkhus, that you went to the dyers' drying-ground and stole a dyers' bundle?" "It is true, Blessed One." The Buddha, the Blessed One, rebuked them: "It is unbecoming, foolish men, unsuitable, improper, unworthy of recluses, not allowable, not to be done. How can you, foolish men, steal a dyers' bundle! This, foolish men, is not for the confidence of those without confidence, nor for the increase of those with confidence; rather, foolish men, it is for the lack of confidence in those without confidence and for wavering in some of those with confidence." Then the Blessed One, having rebuked the bhikkhus of the group of six in many ways, having spoken of being difficult to support... and so on... having praised the arousing of energy, and having given the bhikkhus a talk on Dhamma befitting and suitable to the occasion, addressed the bhikkhus... and so on... "And thus, bhikkhus, you should recite this training rule:

[91] "Whatever bhikkhu should take, by way of theft, what is not given, from a village or from the forest — such a taking of what is not given as when kings, having seized a thief, would execute him or bind him or banish him, saying 'You are a thief, you are a fool, you are deluded, you are a robber' — a bhikkhu taking in such a way what is not given is also one who has incurred an offence entailing defeat, and is not in communion."

[92] "Whatever" means: of whatever sort... and so on... "bhikkhu" means:... and so on... this is the kind of bhikkhu intended in this meaning. "Village" means: a village of one hut is a village, a village of two huts is a village, a village of three huts is a village, a village of four huts is a village, a village with people is a village, a village without people is a village, an enclosed village is a village, an unenclosed village is a village, a place settled as a cattle-station is a village; also a caravan that has settled for more than four months, that too is called a village. "Village precincts" means: in the case of an enclosed village, the range of a clod thrown by a man of average build standing at the gateway-post; in the case of an unenclosed village, the range of a clod thrown by a man of average build standing at the precincts of the house. "Forest" means: setting aside the village and the village precincts, the rest is called forest. "Not given" means: what is not given, not relinquished, not abandoned, guarded, protected, treated as one's own, owned by another. This is called "not given." "By way of theft" means: with thievish intent, with intent to take away. "Should take" means: should take, should carry off, should take away, should disturb its position, should remove it from its place, should let pass a marked spot. "Such" means: a pāda, or the worth of a pāda, or more than a pāda. "Kings" means: a sovereign over the earth, a regional king, district rulers, local chiefs, judges, chief ministers, or those who in governing inflict cutting and breaking. These are called kings. "Thief" means: whoever takes by way of theft what is not given, to the value of five māsakas or more than five māsakas. This is called a thief. "Would execute" means: would strike with the hand, or with the foot, or with a whip, or with a cane, or with a cudgel, or would execute by cutting off. "Would bind" means: would bind with a rope-binding, or with a fetter-binding, or with a chain-binding, or with confinement to a house, or confinement to a city, or confinement to a village, or confinement to a market-town, or would set a man as a guard over him. "Would banish" means: would banish from a village, or from a market-town, or from a city, or from a district, or from a region. "You are a thief, you are a fool, you are deluded, you are a robber" — this is abuse. "In such a way" means: a pāda, or the worth of a pāda, or more than a pāda. "Taking" means: taking, carrying off, taking away, disturbing its position, removing it from its place, letting it pass a marked spot. "Also" is said with reference to the previous case. "Is one who has incurred an offence entailing defeat": just as a withered leaf freed from its stalk is incapable of becoming green again, so too a bhikkhu who has taken by way of theft what is not given, to the value of a pāda or the worth of a pāda or more than a pāda, is not a recluse, not a son of the Sakyans. Therefore it is said: "is one who has incurred an offence entailing defeat." "Not in communion": communion means a single formal act, a single recitation, an equal training. This is called communion. He has none of that with them. Therefore it is said: "is not in communion."

[93] Goods placed in the ground, goods placed on the ground, goods in the air, goods suspended aloft, goods in water, goods in a boat, goods in a vehicle, goods as a load, goods in a monastery park, goods in a dwelling, goods in a field, goods on a site, goods in a village, goods in the forest, water, tooth-wood, a forest tree, goods being carried, a deposit, evasion of a toll-house, a living being, the footless, the two-footed, the four-footed, the many-footed, a spy, a warehouse-keeper, theft by arrangement, making an appointment, making a sign.

[94] "Goods placed in the ground" means: goods deposited in the ground, buried, concealed. Thinking, "I shall steal goods placed in the ground," with thievish intent he seeks an accomplice, or seeks a spade or a basket, or goes there: an offence of wrong-doing. He cuts a stick or a creeper growing there: an offence of wrong-doing. He digs there, or heaps up the soil, or lifts it out: an offence of wrong-doing. He touches the pot: an offence of wrong-doing. He makes it move: a grave offence. He shifts it from its place: an offence entailing defeat. Having inserted his own vessel, with thievish intent he touches goods worth five māsakas or more than five māsakas: an offence of wrong-doing. He makes them move: a grave offence. He puts them into his own vessel or takes a fistful: an offence entailing defeat. With thievish intent he touches goods strung on a thread — a string of ornaments, or a neck-string, or a waist-string, or a cloth, or a turban: an offence of wrong-doing. He makes it move: a grave offence. Taking hold of the end, he raises it: a grave offence. He draws it out, rubbing it against the pot: a grave offence. If he removes it from the mouth of the pot by even so much as a hair's breadth: an offence entailing defeat. With thievish intent, at one effort, he drinks ghee or oil or honey or molasses worth five māsakas or more than five māsakas: an offence entailing defeat. If right there he breaks it, or throws it away, or burns it, or makes it unusable: an offence of wrong-doing.

[95] "Goods placed on the ground" means: goods deposited on the ground. Thinking, "I shall steal goods placed on the ground," with thievish intent he seeks an accomplice or goes there: an offence of wrong-doing. He touches them: an offence of wrong-doing. He makes them move: a grave offence. He shifts them from their place: an offence entailing defeat.

[96] "Goods in the air" means: goods that have gone into the air — a peacock, or a guinea-fowl, or a partridge, or a quail; or a cloth, or a turban, or money, or gold that, being cut loose, is falling. Thinking, "I shall steal goods in the air," with thievish intent he seeks an accomplice or goes there: an offence of wrong-doing. He interrupts its flight: an offence of wrong-doing. He touches it: an offence of wrong-doing. He makes it move: a grave offence. He shifts it from its place: an offence entailing defeat.

[87] 那時，摩揭陀大臣雨勢婆羅門於王舍城巡視工事，往木材倉庫的管理人處；到已，對木材倉庫的管理人這樣說：「喂，那些為修繕城市、以備急難而存放的王家倉庫木材，那些木材在何處？」「主人，那些木材是大王賜給陶師子陀尼耶尊者的。」於是摩揭陀大臣雨勢婆羅門心中不悅：「大王怎麼會把為修繕城市、以備急難而存放的王家倉庫木材，賜給陶師子陀尼耶呢！」於是摩揭陀大臣雨勢婆羅門往摩揭陀王斯尼耶頻婆娑羅處；到已，對摩揭陀王斯尼耶頻婆娑羅這樣說：「大王，聽說大王把為修繕城市、以備急難而存放的王家倉庫木材，賜給了陶師子陀尼耶，是真的嗎？」「是誰這樣說的？」「大王，是木材倉庫的管理人。」「婆羅門，那麼就下令拘捕木材倉庫的管理人。」於是摩揭陀大臣雨勢婆羅門下令綁縛木材倉庫的管理人。陶師子陀尼耶尊者見到木材倉庫的管理人被綁縛押送。見已，對木材倉庫的管理人這樣說：「賢友，你為何被綁縛押送？」「大德，為了那些木材的事。」「賢友，你且去，我也就來。」「大德，請在我被處死之前來到。」

[88] 於是陶師子陀尼耶尊者往摩揭陀王斯尼耶頻婆娑羅的宮殿；到已，坐於所設之座。那時摩揭陀王斯尼耶頻婆娑羅往陶師子陀尼耶尊者處；到已，禮拜陶師子陀尼耶尊者後坐於一面。坐於一面的摩揭陀王斯尼耶頻婆娑羅對陶師子陀尼耶尊者這樣說：「大德，聽說我把為修繕城市、以備急難而存放的王家倉庫木材賜給了尊者，是真的嗎？」「大王，正是如此。」「大德，我們身為國王，事務繁多、所作繁多，縱使曾施與也可能不記得；大德，請讓我憶起吧。」「大王，你可記得，於初即位灌頂時曾說過這樣的話——『凡沙門婆羅門，我已施與，任其受用草、木、水』？」「大德，我記得。大德，有些沙門婆羅門有慚愧、有追悔、好學戒，他們即使對微小之事也生起追悔。我那話是針對他們而說的，而且是指林野中無主之物。大德，你竟以此為藉口，想要取走不與之木材！像我這樣的人，怎麼會殺害、綁縛或驅逐住在我國境內的沙門或婆羅門呢！大德，你去吧，你僅憑毛髮之差而得脫。切莫再作這樣的事。」人們譏嫌、非難、責備：「這些釋子沙門無慚愧、破戒、妄語。他們竟自稱行法、行正、行梵行、說真實語、具戒、善法！他們沒有沙門法，他們沒有梵行法。他們的沙門法已失，他們的梵行法已失。他們哪裡有沙門法，哪裡有梵行法！他們已離沙門法，已離梵行法。他們連國王都欺誑，何況其他人！」諸比丘聽到那些人譏嫌、非難、責備。那些少欲、知足、有慚愧、有追悔、好學戒的比丘們譏嫌、非難、責備：「陶師子陀尼耶尊者怎麼可以盜取國王不與的木材呢！」於是那些比丘以種種方便呵責陶師子陀尼耶尊者後，把此事稟告世尊。於是世尊因此因緣、此事由，令比丘僧集會，問陶師子陀尼耶尊者：「陀尼耶，聽說你取國王不與之木材，是真的嗎？」「世尊，是真的。」佛世尊呵責：「愚癡人，此非所宜、非隨順、不相稱、非沙門所行、不淨、不應作。愚癡人，你怎麼可以取國王不與之木材！愚癡人，此非令未信者生信、已信者增長；愚癡人，反而令未信者不生信、已信者中一部分人轉異。」那時，有一位從前任司法之大臣已於比丘中出家，坐在離世尊不遠處。於是世尊對那比丘這樣說：「比丘，摩揭陀王斯尼耶頻婆娑羅捉到盜賊時，達到多少價值才殺害、綁縛或驅逐？」「世尊，一錢，或值一錢者。」那時在王舍城，五摩沙迦為一錢。於是世尊以種種方便呵責陶師子陀尼耶尊者，說難養……乃至……「諸比丘，當如是誦此學處——

[89] 「若比丘，以盜心取不與之物，如是不與取，諸王捉得盜賊便殺、或縛、或驅逐，說『你是賊、你是愚人、你是迷者、你是竊盜』：比丘如是取不與之物者，此亦波羅夷，不共住。」世尊如是為諸比丘制立學處。

[90] 那時，六群比丘前往染衣者的曬布場，盜取染衣者的包裹，帶回園中分配。諸比丘這樣說：「賢友們，你們福德大啊，你們得到了許多衣。」「賢友，我們哪有福德，我們剛去染衣者的曬布場盜取了染衣者的包裹。」「賢友，世尊不是已制立學處了嗎？賢友，你們為何盜取染衣者的包裹？」「賢友，世尊確實已制立學處，但那是指在村中，不是在林野。」「賢友，那不是一樣嗎？賢友，此非所宜、非隨順、不相稱、非沙門所行、不淨、不應作。賢友，你們怎麼可以盜取染衣者的包裹！賢友，此非令未信者生信、已信者增長；賢友，反而令未信者不生信、已信者中一部分人轉異。」於是那些比丘以種種方便呵責六群比丘後，把此事稟告世尊。於是世尊因此因緣、此事由，令比丘僧集會，問六群比丘：「諸比丘，聽說你們前往染衣者的曬布場，盜取染衣者的包裹，是真的嗎？」「世尊，是真的。」佛世尊呵責：「愚癡人們，此非所宜、非隨順、不相稱、非沙門所行、不淨、不應作。愚癡人們，你們怎麼可以盜取染衣者的包裹！愚癡人們，此非令未信者生信、已信者增長；愚癡人們，反而令未信者不生信、已信者中一部分人轉異。」於是世尊以種種方便呵責六群比丘，說難養……乃至……讚歎發勤精進，為諸比丘作與此相宜、與此隨順的法語後，告諸比丘……乃至……「諸比丘，當如是誦此學處——

[91] 「若比丘，於村中或林野中，以盜心取不與之物，如是不與取，諸王捉得盜賊便殺、或縛、或驅逐，說『你是賊、你是愚人、你是迷者、你是竊盜』：比丘如是取不與之物者，此亦波羅夷，不共住。」

[92] 「若」者，凡如是者……乃至……「比丘」者……乃至……此處所意指者即此比丘。「村」者：一屋亦名村，二屋亦名村，三屋亦名村，四屋亦名村，有人之處亦名村，無人之處亦名村，有圍牆者亦名村，無圍牆者亦名村，牛群棲止之處亦名村，凡商隊駐留超過四個月者亦稱為村。「村界」者：有圍牆之村，指中等男子立於門柱處所擲土塊所及之處；無圍牆之村，指中等男子立於屋界處所擲土塊所及之處。「林野」者：除村及村界外，其餘皆名林野。「不與」者：凡未施與、未放捨、未棄捨、受守護、受看守、被視為己有、為他人所攝持者，此名不與。「以盜心」者：即盜賊之心、欲竊取之心。「取」者：取、持、竊、壞其威儀路、移離本處、越過約定之處。「如是」者：一錢、或值一錢者、或超過一錢者。「諸王」者：統治大地之王、地方王、領邑主、中間領主、裁判官、大臣，或凡施行斷截破壞之刑而治理者，此等名為諸王。「賊」者：凡以盜心取值五摩沙迦或超過五摩沙迦之不與物者，此名為賊。「便殺」者：或以手、或以足、或以鞭、或以杖、或以半棍、或斬斷而殺。「或縛」者：或以繩縛、或以鐐縛、或以鎖鏈縛、或禁閉於家、或禁閉於城、或禁閉於村、或禁閉於鎮，或令人看守。「或驅逐」者：從村、或從鎮、或從城、或從國、或從國之一區驅逐出去。「你是賊、你是愚人、你是迷者、你是竊盜」者，此是呵罵之語。「如是」者：一錢、或值一錢者、或超過一錢者。「取者」：取著、持著、竊著、壞其威儀路、移離本處、越過約定之處。「此亦」者，是就前條而言。「是波羅夷」者：猶如黃葉脫離枝幹，不可能再青翠，如是比丘以盜心取一錢、或值一錢者、或超過一錢之不與物，即非沙門、非釋子。因此說「是波羅夷」。「不共住」者：共住名為同一羯磨、同一說戒、同等學處，此名共住；他與之無有此事，因此說「不共住」。

[93] 地中物、陸上物、空中物、懸空物、水中物、船中物、車中物、擔中物、園中物、寺中物、田中物、宅地物、村中物、林野中物、水、齒木、林樹、他人攜行之物、寄託物、關稅處、有命者、無足者、二足者、四足者、多足者、偵探者、代人守護者、共謀竊取、約定作、示相作。

[94] 「地中物」者：財物存放於地中、埋藏、覆蓋。若以盜心思「我要盜取地中之物」而尋覓同伴、或尋覓鋤頭或籃子、或前往，突吉羅。若砍斷生於其上之木或蔓藤，突吉羅。若於其處掘土、或撥土、或取出土，突吉羅。若觸摸甕，突吉羅。若使之動搖，偷蘭遮。若移離本處，波羅夷。若將自己的容器放入其中，以盜心觸摸值五摩沙迦或超過五摩沙迦之物，突吉羅。使之動搖，偷蘭遮。若使之入於自己的容器中、或截取一握，波羅夷。若對貫於線上之物——珠串、或頸飾線、或腰飾線、或衣片、或纏頭布——以盜心觸摸，突吉羅。使之動搖，偷蘭遮。捉住一端舉起，偷蘭遮。摩擦著拉出，偷蘭遮。乃至僅使一髮端之量離開甕口，波羅夷。若以盜心一次動作飲下值五摩沙迦或超過五摩沙迦之酥、或油、或蜜、或糖蜜，波羅夷。若就於其處打破、或倒棄、或燒毀、或使之不堪受用，突吉羅。

[95] 「陸上物」者：財物置放於地面之上。若以盜心思「我要盜取陸上之物」而尋覓同伴、或前往，突吉羅。觸摸，突吉羅。使之動搖，偷蘭遮。移離本處，波羅夷。

[96] 「空中物」者：財物處於空中。如孔雀、或鷓鴣、或雉、或鶉，或衣片、或纏頭布、或銀、或金，正墜落之時。若以盜心思「我要盜取空中之物」而尋覓同伴、或前往，突吉羅。截斷其飛行，突吉羅。觸摸，突吉羅。使之動搖，偷蘭遮。移離本處，波羅夷。

Pārājiko hotīti seyyathāpi nāma paṇḍupalāso bandhanā pavutto abhabbo haritatthāya , evameva bhikkhu pādaṃ vā pādārahaṃ vā atirekapādaṃ vā adinnaṃ theyyasaṅkhātaṃ ādiyitvā assamaṇo hoti asakyaputtiyo. Tena vuccati – ‘pārājiko hotī’ti. Asaṃvāsoti saṃvāso nāma ekakammaṃ ekuddeso samasikkhatā. Eso saṃvāso nāma. So tena saddhiṃ natthi. Tena vuccati – ‘asaṃvāso’ti.

[93] Bhūmaṭṭhaṃ thalaṭṭhaṃ ākāsaṭṭhaṃ vehāsaṭṭhaṃ udakaṭṭhaṃ nāvaṭṭhaṃ yānaṭṭhaṃ bhāraṭṭhaṃ ārāmaṭṭhaṃ vihāraṭṭhaṃ khettaṭṭhaṃ vatthuṭṭhaṃ gāmaṭṭhaṃ araññaṭṭhaṃ udakaṃ dantapoṇaṃ vanappati haraṇakaṃ upanidhi suṅkaghātaṃ pāṇo apadaṃ dvipadaṃ catuppadaṃ bahuppadaṃ ocarako oṇirakkho saṃvidāvahāro saṅketakammaṃ nimittakammanti.

[94] Bhūmaṭṭhaṃ nāma bhaṇḍaṃ bhūmiyaṃ nikkhittaṃ hoti nikhātaṃ paṭicchannaṃ. Bhūmaṭṭhaṃ bhaṇḍaṃ avaharissāmīti theyyacitto dutiyaṃ vā pariyesati kudālaṃ vā piṭakaṃ vā pariyesati gacchati vā, āpatti dukkaṭassa. Tattha jātakaṃ kaṭṭhaṃ vā lataṃ vā chindati, āpatti dukkaṭassa. Tattha paṃsuṃ khaṇati vā byūhati vā uddharati vā, āpatti dukkaṭassa. Kumbhiṃ āmasati, āpatti dukkaṭassa. Phandāpeti, āpatti thullaccayassa. Ṭhānā cāveti, āpatti pārājikassa. Attano bhājanaṃ pavesetvā pañcamāsakaṃ vā atirekapañcamāsakaṃ vā agghanakaṃ theyyacitto āmasati, āpatti dukkaṭassa. Phandāpeti, āpatti thullaccayassa. Attano bhājanagataṃ vā karoti muṭṭhiṃ vā chindati, āpatti pārājikassa. Suttāruḷhaṃ bhaṇḍaṃ pāmaṅgaṃ vā kaṇṭhasuttakaṃ vā kaṭisuttakaṃ vā sāṭakaṃ vā veṭhanaṃ vā theyyacitto āmasati, āpatti dukkaṭassa. Phandāpeti, āpatti thullaccayassa. Koṭiyaṃ gahetvā uccāreti, āpatti thullaccayassa. Ghaṃsanto nīharati, āpatti thullaccayassa. Antamaso kesaggamattampi kumbhimukhā moceti, āpatti pārājikassa. Sappiṃ vā telaṃ vā madhuṃ vā phāṇitaṃ vā pañcamāsakaṃ vā atirekapañcamāsakaṃ vā agghanakaṃ theyyacitto ekena payogena pivati, āpatti pārājikassa. Tattheva bhindati vā chaḍḍeti vā jhāpeti vā aparibhogaṃ vā karoti, āpatti dukkaṭassa.

[95] Thalaṭṭhaṃ nāma bhaṇḍaṃ thale nikkhittaṃ hoti. Thalaṭṭhaṃ bhaṇḍaṃ avaharissāmīti theyyacitto dutiyaṃ vā pariyesati gacchati vā, āpatti dukkaṭassa. Āmasati, āpatti dukkaṭassa. Phandāpeti, āpatti thullaccayassa. Ṭhānā cāveti, āpatti pārājikassa.

[96] Ākāsaṭṭhaṃ nāma bhaṇḍaṃ ākāsagataṃ hoti. Moro vā kapiñjaro vā tittiro vā vaṭṭako vā, sāṭakaṃ vā veṭhanaṃ vā hiraññaṃ vā suvaṇṇaṃ vā chijjamānaṃ patati. Ākāsaṭṭhaṃ bhaṇḍaṃ avaharissāmīti theyyacitto dutiyaṃ vā pariyesati gacchati vā, āpatti dukkaṭassa. Gamanaṃ upacchindati, āpatti dukkaṭassa. Āmasati, āpatti dukkaṭassa. Phandāpeti, āpatti thullaccayassa. Ṭhānā cāveti, āpatti pārājikassa.
