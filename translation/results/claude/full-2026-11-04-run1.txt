Pārājikapāḷi — 1. Pārājikakaṇḍaṃ
vin01m.mul.xml, verses 203-207

[203] In three ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I am an obtainer of the first jhāna," there is an offence entailing defeat: beforehand he thinks, "I shall speak falsely"; while speaking he thinks, "I am speaking falsely"; after speaking he thinks, "I have spoken falsely." In four ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I am an obtainer of the first jhāna," there is an offence entailing defeat: beforehand he thinks, "I shall speak falsely"; while speaking he thinks, "I am speaking falsely"; after speaking he thinks, "I have spoken falsely"; he misrepresents his view. In five ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I am an obtainer of the first jhāna," there is an offence entailing defeat: beforehand he thinks, "I shall speak falsely"; while speaking he thinks, "I am speaking falsely"; after speaking he thinks, "I have spoken falsely"; he misrepresents his view; he misrepresents his belief. In six ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I am an obtainer of the first jhāna," there is an offence entailing defeat: beforehand he thinks, "I shall speak falsely"; while speaking he thinks, "I am speaking falsely"; after speaking he thinks, "I have spoken falsely"; he misrepresents his view; he misrepresents his belief; he misrepresents his preference. In seven ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I am an obtainer of the first jhāna," there is an offence entailing defeat: beforehand he thinks, "I shall speak falsely"; while speaking he thinks, "I am speaking falsely"; after speaking he thinks, "I have spoken falsely"; he misrepresents his view; he misrepresents his belief; he misrepresents his preference; he misrepresents his state.

[204] In three ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I am a master of the first jhāna," there is an offence entailing defeat: beforehand he thinks, "I shall speak falsely"; while speaking he thinks, "I am speaking falsely"; after speaking he thinks, "I have spoken falsely." In four ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I am a master of the first jhāna," there is an offence entailing defeat: beforehand he thinks, "I shall speak falsely"; while speaking he thinks, "I am speaking falsely"; after speaking he thinks, "I have spoken falsely"; he misrepresents his view. In five ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I am a master of the first jhāna," there is an offence entailing defeat: beforehand he thinks, "I shall speak falsely"; while speaking he thinks, "I am speaking falsely"; after speaking he thinks, "I have spoken falsely"; he misrepresents his view; he misrepresents his belief. In six ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I am a master of the first jhāna," there is an offence entailing defeat: beforehand he thinks, "I shall speak falsely"; while speaking he thinks, "I am speaking falsely"; after speaking he thinks, "I have spoken falsely"; he misrepresents his view; he misrepresents his belief; he misrepresents his preference. In seven ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I am a master of the first jhāna," there is an offence entailing defeat: beforehand he thinks, "I shall speak falsely"; while speaking he thinks, "I am speaking falsely"; after speaking he thinks, "I have spoken falsely"; he misrepresents his view; he misrepresents his belief; he misrepresents his preference; he misrepresents his state.

[205] In three ways, when one deliberately lies saying "The first jhāna has been realised by me," there is an offence entailing defeat: beforehand he thinks, "I shall speak falsely"; while speaking he thinks, "I am speaking falsely"; after speaking he thinks, "I have spoken falsely." In four ways, when one deliberately lies saying "The first jhāna has been realised by me," there is an offence entailing defeat: beforehand he thinks, "I shall speak falsely"; while speaking he thinks, "I am speaking falsely"; after speaking he thinks, "I have spoken falsely"; he misrepresents his view. In five ways, when one deliberately lies saying "The first jhāna has been realised by me," there is an offence entailing defeat: beforehand he thinks, "I shall speak falsely"; while speaking he thinks, "I am speaking falsely"; after speaking he thinks, "I have spoken falsely"; he misrepresents his view; he misrepresents his belief. In six ways, when one deliberately lies saying "The first jhāna has been realised by me," there is an offence entailing defeat: beforehand he thinks, "I shall speak falsely"; while speaking he thinks, "I am speaking falsely"; after speaking he thinks, "I have spoken falsely"; he misrepresents his view; he misrepresents his belief; he misrepresents his preference. In seven ways, when one deliberately lies saying "The first jhāna has been realised by me," there is an offence entailing defeat: beforehand he thinks, "I shall speak falsely"; while speaking he thinks, "I am speaking falsely"; after speaking he thinks, "I have spoken falsely"; he misrepresents his view; he misrepresents his belief; he misrepresents his preference; he misrepresents his state. As this first jhāna has been set out in full, so should the whole of it be set out in full.

[206] In three ways… and so on… in seven ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I attained the second jhāna"… "the third jhāna"… "the fourth jhāna," "I am attaining it," "I have attained it," "I am an obtainer of the fourth jhāna," "I am a master of it," "The fourth jhāna has been realised by me," there is an offence entailing defeat. Beforehand he thinks, "I shall speak falsely"; while speaking he thinks, "I am speaking falsely"; after speaking he thinks, "I have spoken falsely"; he misrepresents his view; he misrepresents his belief; he misrepresents his preference; he misrepresents his state.

[207] In three ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I attained the empty liberation"… "the signless liberation"… "the undirected liberation," "I am attaining it," "I have attained it," "I am an obtainer of the undirected liberation," "I am a master of it," "The undirected liberation has been realised by me," there is an offence entailing defeat… and so on. In three ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I attained empty concentration"… "signless concentration"… "undirected concentration," "I am attaining it," "I have attained it," "I am an obtainer of undirected concentration," "I am a master of it," "Undirected concentration has been realised by me," there is an offence entailing defeat. In three ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I attained the empty attainment"… "the signless attainment"… "the undirected attainment," "I am attaining it," "I have attained it," "I am an obtainer of the undirected attainment," "I am a master of it," "The undirected attainment has been realised by me," there is an offence entailing defeat. In three ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I attained the three true knowledges," "I am attaining them," "I have attained them," "I am an obtainer of the three true knowledges," "I am a master of them," "The three true knowledges have been realised by me," there is an offence entailing defeat. In three ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I attained the four foundations of mindfulness"… "the four right strivings"… "the four bases of psychic power," "I am attaining them," "I have attained them," "I am an obtainer of the four bases of psychic power," "I am a master of them," "The four bases of psychic power have been realised by me," there is an offence entailing defeat. In three ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I attained the five faculties"… "the five powers," "I am attaining them," "I have attained them," "I am an obtainer of the five powers," "I am a master of them," "The five powers have been realised by me," there is an offence entailing defeat. In three ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I attained the seven factors of enlightenment," "I am attaining them," "I have attained them," "I am an obtainer of the seven factors of enlightenment," "I am a master of them," "The seven factors of enlightenment have been realised by me," there is an offence entailing defeat. In three ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I attained the noble eightfold path," "I am attaining it," "I have attained it," "I am an obtainer of the noble eightfold path," "I am a master of it," "The noble eightfold path has been realised by me," there is an offence entailing defeat. In three ways, when one deliberately lies saying "I attained the fruit of stream-entry"… "the fruit of once-returning"… "the fruit of non-returning"… "arahantship," "I am attaining it," "I have attained it," "I am an obtainer of arahantship," "I am a master of it," "Arahantship has been realised by me," there is an offence entailing defeat. In three ways, when one deliberately lies saying "Lust has been relinquished by me, vomited out, released, abandoned, given up, cast off, utterly cast off," there is an offence entailing defeat. In three ways, when one deliberately lies saying "Hatred has been relinquished by me, vomited out, released, abandoned, given up, cast off, utterly cast off," there is an offence entailing defeat. In three ways, when one deliberately lies saying "Delusion has been relinquished by me, vomited out, released, abandoned, given up, cast off, utterly cast off," there is an offence entailing defeat. In three ways, when one deliberately lies saying "My mind is unobstructed by lust," there is an offence entailing defeat. In three ways, when one deliberately lies saying "My mind is unobstructed by hatred," there is an offence entailing defeat. In three ways… and so on… in seven ways, when one deliberately lies saying "My mind is unobstructed by delusion," there is an offence entailing defeat: beforehand he thinks, "I shall speak falsely"; while speaking he thinks, "I am speaking falsely"; after speaking he thinks, "I have spoken falsely"; he misrepresents his view; he misrepresents his belief; he misrepresents his preference; he misrepresents his state.
