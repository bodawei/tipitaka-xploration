Pārājikapāḷi — 1. Pārājikakaṇḍaṃ
vin01m.mul.xml, section verses 210-213 (condensed prose summary)

Here the exposition continues with the permutations of the fourth pārājika: a bhikkhu who deliberately lies in claiming, in the threefold manner — "I attained," "I attain," "I have attained" — or "I am a gainer of," "I am a master of," "it has been realised by me," commits an offence entailing defeat. This holds when he pairs the second jhāna with the third, or with the fourth, or with each of the empty, signless and undirected liberations, concentrations and attainments, with the three true knowledges, the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right strivings, the four bases of psychic power, the five faculties, the five powers, the seven factors of enlightenment, the noble eightfold path, and the fruits of stream-entry, once-returning, non-returning and arahantship; likewise when he adds that lust, hatred or delusion has been given up by him — vomited, released, abandoned, relinquished, discarded, utterly discarded — or that his mind is unobstructed by lust, hatred or delusion. The same applies with the second jhāna combined with the first, and so on; the formula is extended through all the modes up to the sevenfold, concluding with "he misrepresents his view, his belief, his preference, his state." Taking each item in turn as the root, the wheel of combinations is to be turned through in full.

Thus the third jhāna is paired in the same way with the fourth, and so on up to arahantship and the abandoning of lust, hatred and delusion, and also back with the first and second jhānas; in every case deliberate false speech entails an offence entailing defeat. Likewise beginning from "my mind is unobstructed by delusion," paired in turn with the first, second, third and fourth jhānas, with the empty, signless and undirected liberations, concentrations and attainments, with the three true knowledges, the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right strivings, the four bases of psychic power, the five faculties, the five powers, the seven factors of enlightenment, the noble eightfold path, and the four fruits up to arahantship, and with the claim that lust, hatred and delusion have been given up, or that the mind is unobstructed by lust or hatred — in each case, spoken as a deliberate lie, it is an offence entailing defeat. In all these, the liar knows beforehand "I shall speak falsely," while speaking "I am speaking falsely," and afterwards "I spoke falsely," and he misrepresents his view, his belief, his preference and his state. Just as the single-rooted series has been set out in full, so too the two-rooted and the rest are to be expanded.
