Pārājikapāḷi — 1. Pārājikakaṇḍaṃ
vin01m.mul.xml, section verses 214 (condensed prose summary)

Through three up to seven modes, if a bhikkhu knowingly speaks falsely, claiming that he has attained, is attaining, or has attained the first, second, third or fourth jhāna; the empty, signless and undirected liberations; the empty, signless and undirected concentrations; the empty, signless and undirected attainments; the three true knowledges; the four foundations of mindfulness, four right strivings, four bases of psychic power, five faculties, five powers, seven factors of enlightenment, the noble eightfold path; the fruits of stream-entry, once-returning, non-returning, and arahantship; or claiming that lust, hatred and delusion have been given up by him — vomited, released, abandoned, relinquished, cast off and thrown away — and that his mind is unobstructed by lust, hatred and delusion, there is an offence entailing defeat. The modes are: beforehand he knows he will speak falsely; while speaking he knows he is speaking falsely; after speaking he knows he has spoken falsely; and he misrepresents his view, his belief, his preference, and his state.
