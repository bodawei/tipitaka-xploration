Pārājikapāḷi — 1. Pārājikakaṇḍaṃ
vin01m.mul.xml, section verses 215 (condensed prose summary)

Continuing the exposition of the fourth offence entailing defeat, the text runs through the permutations of false claims to superhuman states: a bhikkhu who, wishing to say "I attained the first jhāna," deliberately says instead "I attained the second jhāna" — and likewise the third and the fourth — commits an offence entailing defeat if the hearer understands him, a grave offence if the hearer does not. The same threefold, and by extension sevenfold, analysis is applied in turn to every other attainment that may be substituted: the empty, signless and undirected liberations, concentrations and attainments; the three true knowledges; the four foundations of mindfulness, four right strivings and four bases of psychic power; the five faculties and five powers; the seven factors of enlightenment; the noble eightfold path; the fruits of stream-entry, once-returning and non-returning, and arahantship. Likewise for claims of the abandoning of the defilements — "lust is given up by me, hatred is given up by me, delusion is given up by me, vomited, released, abandoned, relinquished, cast out, utterly cast out" — and claims that his mind is unobstructed by lust, by hatred, by delusion. In each case, if the hearer understands there is an offence entailing defeat; if he does not understand, a grave offence. The threefold factor in every instance is that beforehand he knows he will speak falsely, while speaking he knows he is speaking falsely, and afterwards he knows he has spoken falsely; and in doing so he misrepresents his view, his belief, his preference and his state.
