Pārājikapāḷi — 1. Pārājikakaṇḍaṃ
vin01m.mul.xml, section verses 216-217 (condensed prose summary)

The text continues the mechanical permutation of false claims to superhuman states. In each case a bhikkhu, intending to say that he has attained one state — the second jhāna, say, or that his mind is unobstructed by delusion — deliberately utters a falsehood naming instead another state: the third jhāna, the fourth jhāna, the first jhāna, or freedom from hatred, and so on through the whole series. If the hearer understands him, the offence is one entailing defeat; if the hearer does not understand, the offence is a grave one. The same analysis is applied through three modes, then four, five, six and seven modes, in each case including the clauses in which he misrepresents his view, his belief, his preference and his state. The permutations are thus extended by substituting each claimed attainment for every other in turn, the legal result remaining constant throughout.
