Pārājikapāḷi — 1. Pārājikakaṇḍaṃ
vin01m.mul.xml, section verses 218-219 (condensed prose summary)

Expanding the same analysis in three to seven modes, the text runs the claim of superhuman states through every permutation: a bhikkhu who deliberately lies that he has attained the first, second, third and fourth jhānas, the empty, signless and undirected liberations, the corresponding concentrations and attainments, the three true knowledges, the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right strivings, the four bases of psychic power, the five faculties, the five powers, the seven factors of enlightenment, the noble eightfold path, the fruits of stream-entry, once-returning, non-returning, and arahantship; or that lust, hatred and delusion have been cast off by him — vomited, released, abandoned, relinquished, discarded and wholly discarded — or that his mind is unobstructed by lust, hatred and delusion: if the hearer understands, it is an offence entailing defeat; if the hearer does not understand, it is a grave offence. The same ruling applies in each case where he wishes to say one thing but says another — wishing to say that his mind is unobstructed by delusion, he says he attained the first jhāna; wishing to say he attained the first jhāna, he says he attained the second; wishing to say his mind is unobstructed by lust, he says it is unobstructed by hatred — through every such interchange of terms. In all these instances the defining factors are present: beforehand he knows "I shall speak falsely," while speaking he knows "I am speaking falsely," and afterwards he knows "I have spoken falsely," and he misrepresents his view, his belief, his preference and his state.
