Pārājikapāḷi — 1. Pārājikakaṇḍaṃ
vin01m.mul.xml, verses 162-171

[162] At that time the Buddha, the Blessed One, was dwelling at Vesālī, in the Great Wood, in the Hall with the Peaked Roof. Now at that time the Blessed One spoke to the bhikkhus in many ways on the subject of the unattractive, he spoke in praise of the unattractive, he spoke in praise of the development of the perception of the unattractive, he spoke in praise of the attainment based on the unattractive, explaining it again and again. Then the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus: "Bhikkhus, I wish to retire for half a month. No one should approach me, except the one who brings me almsfood." "Very good, venerable sir," those bhikkhus replied to the Blessed One, and then no one approached the Blessed One except the one who brought him almsfood. The bhikkhus, thinking, "The Blessed One speaks to the bhikkhus in many ways on the subject of the unattractive, he speaks in praise of the unattractive, he speaks in praise of the development of the perception of the unattractive, he speaks in praise of the attainment based on the unattractive, explaining it again and again," dwelt devoted to the pursuit of the development of the perception of the unattractive in many ways and forms. They were repelled by, ashamed of, and disgusted with their own bodies. Just as a woman or a man, young, youthful, fond of adornment, having bathed the head, would be repelled by, ashamed of, and disgusted with a snake's corpse, a dog's corpse, or a human corpse hung around the neck, even so those bhikkhus, repelled by, ashamed of, and disgusted with their own bodies, deprived themselves of life, deprived one another of life, and approached Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse, saying: "Good friend, please deprive us of life. This bowl and robe will be yours." Then Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse, hired by bowls and robes, deprived many bhikkhus of life, and taking a blood-stained sword, approached the river Vaggumudā.

[163] Then, as Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse was washing that blood-stained sword, remorse arose in him, and regret arose: "Truly, no gain have I, truly no gain have I; truly ill-gotten for me, truly not well-gotten for me. Truly much demerit have I accumulated, in that I have deprived virtuous bhikkhus of good character of life." Then a certain devatā of the Māra host, coming over the unbroken water, said to Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse: "Well done, well done, good sir! Gain have you, good sir, well-gotten for you, good sir. Much merit have you accumulated, good sir, in that you have ferried across those not yet ferried across." Then Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse, thinking, "Gain indeed have I, well-gotten indeed for me, much merit indeed have I accumulated, for indeed I ferry across those not yet ferried across," took his sharp sword, went from dwelling to dwelling, from cloister to cloister, saying: "Who is not yet ferried across? Whom shall I ferry across?" Thereupon those bhikkhus who were not free from lust had fear, had terror, had hair-standing. But those bhikkhus who were free from lust had no fear, no terror, no hair-standing. Then Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse deprived one bhikkhu of life in a single day, deprived two bhikkhus of life in a single day, deprived three bhikkhus of life in a single day, deprived four bhikkhus of life in a single day, deprived five bhikkhus of life in a single day, deprived ten bhikkhus of life in a single day, deprived twenty bhikkhus of life in a single day, deprived thirty bhikkhus of life in a single day, deprived forty bhikkhus of life in a single day, deprived fifty bhikkhus of life in a single day, deprived sixty bhikkhus of life in a single day.

[164] Then the Blessed One, after that half month had passed, rose from his retirement and addressed the venerable Ānanda: "Why, Ānanda, does the Sangha of bhikkhus seem so diminished?" "It is so, venerable sir, because the Blessed One spoke to the bhikkhus in many ways on the subject of the unattractive, spoke in praise of the unattractive, spoke in praise of the development of the perception of the unattractive, spoke in praise of the attainment based on the unattractive, explaining it again and again. And those bhikkhus, venerable sir, thinking, 'The Blessed One speaks to the bhikkhus in many ways on the subject of the unattractive, speaks in praise of the unattractive, speaks in praise of the development of the perception of the unattractive, speaks in praise of the attainment based on the unattractive, explaining it again and again,' dwelt devoted to the pursuit of the development of the perception of the unattractive in many ways and forms. They were repelled by, ashamed of, and disgusted with their own bodies. Just as a woman or a man, young, youthful, fond of adornment, having bathed the head, would be repelled by, ashamed of, and disgusted with a snake's corpse, a dog's corpse, or a human corpse hung around the neck, even so those bhikkhus, repelled by, ashamed of, and disgusted with their own bodies, deprived themselves of life, deprived one another of life, and approached Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse, saying: 'Good friend, please deprive us of life. This bowl and robe will be yours.' Then, venerable sir, Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse, hired by bowls and robes, deprived one bhikkhu of life in a single day… and deprived sixty bhikkhus of life in a single day. Please, venerable sir, let the Blessed One explain another manner by which this Sangha of bhikkhus may attain final knowledge and be established." "In that case, Ānanda, assemble in the assembly hall all the bhikkhus who dwell in dependence on Vesālī." "Very good, venerable sir," the venerable Ānanda replied to the Blessed One, assembled in the assembly hall all the bhikkhus who dwell in dependence on Vesālī, and approached the Blessed One; having approached, he said to the Blessed One: "The Sangha of bhikkhus is assembled, venerable sir. Now let the Blessed One do as he considers timely."

[165] Then the Blessed One approached the assembly hall; having approached, he sat down on the prepared seat. Seated, the Blessed One addressed the bhikkhus: "This concentration through mindfulness of breathing, bhikkhus, when developed and cultivated, is peaceful and sublime, an unadulterated pleasant abiding, and it causes to vanish and subside on the spot the evil unwholesome states that have arisen. Just as, bhikkhus, in the last month of the hot season, when the dust and dirt are stirred up, a great untimely rain-cloud causes it to vanish and subside on the spot, even so, bhikkhus, this concentration through mindfulness of breathing, when developed and cultivated, is peaceful and sublime, an unadulterated pleasant abiding, and it causes to vanish and subside on the spot the evil unwholesome states that have arisen. And how, bhikkhus, is concentration through mindfulness of breathing developed, how is it cultivated, so that it is peaceful and sublime, an unadulterated pleasant abiding, and causes to vanish and subside on the spot the evil unwholesome states that have arisen? Here, bhikkhus, a bhikkhu, gone to the forest, gone to the foot of a tree, or gone to an empty hut, sits down, folding his legs crosswise, setting his body erect, establishing mindfulness in front of him. Mindful, he breathes in; mindful, he breathes out. Breathing in long, he knows, 'I breathe in long'; breathing out long, he knows, 'I breathe out long.' Breathing in short, he knows, 'I breathe in short'; breathing out short, he knows, 'I breathe out short.' He trains thus: 'Experiencing the whole body, I will breathe in'; he trains thus: 'Experiencing the whole body, I will breathe out.' He trains thus: 'Calming the bodily formation, I will breathe in'; he trains thus: 'Calming the bodily formation, I will breathe out.' He trains thus: 'Experiencing rapture, I will breathe in'; he trains thus: 'Experiencing rapture, I will breathe out.' He trains thus: 'Experiencing happiness, I will breathe in'; he trains thus: 'Experiencing happiness, I will breathe out.' He trains thus: 'Experiencing the mental formation, I will breathe in'; he trains thus: 'Experiencing the mental formation, I will breathe out.' He trains thus: 'Calming the mental formation, I will breathe in'; he trains thus: 'Calming the mental formation, I will breathe out.' He trains thus: 'Experiencing the mind, I will breathe in'; he trains thus: 'Experiencing the mind, I will breathe out.' He trains thus: 'Gladdening the mind, I will breathe in'… 'Concentrating the mind, I will breathe in'… 'Liberating the mind, I will breathe in'… 'Contemplating impermanence, I will breathe in'… 'Contemplating dispassion, I will breathe in'… 'Contemplating cessation, I will breathe in'… He trains thus: 'Contemplating relinquishment, I will breathe in'; he trains thus: 'Contemplating relinquishment, I will breathe out.' Thus developed, bhikkhus, concentration through mindfulness of breathing, thus cultivated, is peaceful and sublime, an unadulterated pleasant abiding, and it causes to vanish and subside on the spot the evil unwholesome states that have arisen."

[166] Then the Blessed One, on this ground and occasion, having had the Sangha of bhikkhus assembled, questioned the bhikkhus: "Is it true, bhikkhus, that bhikkhus deprive themselves of life, deprive one another of life, and approach Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse, saying: 'Good friend, please deprive us of life. This bowl and robe will be yours'?" "It is true, Blessed One." The Buddha, the Blessed One, rebuked them: "It is unworthy, bhikkhus, it is unsuitable, improper, not befitting a recluse, not allowable, not to be done, in those bhikkhus. How indeed, bhikkhus, can those bhikkhus deprive themselves of life, deprive one another of life, and approach Migalaṇḍika the sham recluse, saying: 'Good friend, please deprive us of life. This bowl and robe will be yours'? This, bhikkhus, is not for the inspiring of faith in the unconfident… and so, bhikkhus, you should recite this training rule:

[167] 'Whatever bhikkhu intentionally deprives a human being of life, or seeks out a knife-bringer for him, he too is one who has committed an offence entailing defeat, not in communion.' Thus was this training rule laid down for the bhikkhus by the Blessed One.

[168] Now at that time a certain lay follower was sick. His wife was beautiful, good-looking, and lovely. The bhikkhus of the group of six had minds attached to that woman. Then the bhikkhus of the group of six thought: "If this lay follower lives, friends, we will not get that woman. Come, friends, let us praise the beauty of death to that lay follower." Then the bhikkhus of the group of six approached that lay follower; having approached, they said to that lay follower: "You, lay follower, have done good, done skillfully, done what protects from fear, have done no evil, done no cruelty, done no crime. Good has been done by you, evil has not been done by you. Why should you live this wretched evil life? Death is better for you than life. From here, having died, at the breaking up of the body after death, you will be reborn in a good destination, a heavenly world. There you will enjoy yourself supplied and furnished with the five divine cords of sensual pleasure."

[169] Then that lay follower thought: "It is true what the venerable ones say. I have indeed done good, done skillfully, done what protects from fear, have done no evil, done no cruelty, done no crime. Good has been done by me, evil has not been done by me. Why should I live this wretched evil life? Death is better for me than life. From here, having died, at the breaking up of the body after death, I will be reborn in a good destination, a heavenly world. There I will enjoy myself supplied and furnished with the five divine cords of sensual pleasure." So he ate unwholesome foods, chewed unwholesome chewables, tasted unwholesome tastables, and drank unwholesome drinks. As he ate unwholesome foods, chewed unwholesome chewables, tasted unwholesome tastables, and drank unwholesome drinks, a severe illness arose. He died of that illness. His wife complained, grumbled, and reproached: "These recluses, sons of the Sakyans, are shameless, immoral, and speakers of falsehood. They who claim to be practisers of the Dhamma, of even conduct, of the holy life, speakers of truth, virtuous, of good character! There is no recluseship in these, no holy life in these; the recluseship of these is destroyed, the holy life of these is destroyed. How could there be recluseship in these, how could there be holy life in these? These are devoid of recluseship, devoid of holy life. These praised the beauty of death to my husband. My husband was killed by these." Other people too complained, grumbled, and reproached: "These recluses, sons of the Sakyans, are shameless, immoral, and speakers of falsehood. They who claim to be practisers of the Dhamma, of even conduct, of the holy life, speakers of truth, virtuous, of good character! There is no recluseship in these, no holy life in these; the recluseship of these is destroyed, the holy life of these is destroyed. How could there be recluseship in these, how could there be holy life in these? These are devoid of recluseship, devoid of holy life. These praised the beauty of death to the lay follower. The lay follower was killed by these." The bhikkhus heard those people complaining, grumbling, and reproaching. Those bhikkhus who were of few desires… complained, grumbled, and reproached: "How indeed can the bhikkhus of the group of six praise the beauty of death to a lay follower?"

[170] Then those bhikkhus, having rebuked the bhikkhus of the group of six in many ways, reported the matter to the Blessed One… "Is it true, bhikkhus, that you praise the beauty of death to a lay follower?" "It is true, Blessed One." The Buddha, the Blessed One, rebuked them: "It is unworthy, foolish men, it is unsuitable, improper, not befitting a recluse, not allowable, not to be done. How indeed, foolish men, can you praise the beauty of death to a lay follower? This, foolish men, is not for the inspiring of faith in the unconfident… and so, bhikkhus, you should recite this training rule:

[171] 'Whatever bhikkhu intentionally deprives a human being of life, or seeks out a knife-bringer for him, or praises the beauty of death, or urges him on to death, saying: "O man, why should you live this wretched evil life? Death is better for you than life" — thus with a mind bent on that, with intention bent on that, praising the beauty of death in many ways, or urging him on to death, he too is one who has committed an offence entailing defeat, not in communion.'"
