Pārājikapāḷi — 1. Pārājikakaṇḍaṃ

[172] "Who" means whoever, of whatever kind … "bhikkhu" means … here in this connection it is a bhikkhu who is intended. "Intentionally" means knowing, perceiving, deliberately, having overstepped — a transgression. "Human being" means: from the first mind that arises in the mother's womb, the first consciousness that becomes manifest, until the time of death — in this interval that is called a human being. "Deprives of life" means cuts off the life-faculty, obstructs it, disrupts the continuity. "Or should seek a knife-bringer for him" means a sword, or a spear, or a club, or a cudgel, or a stone, or a knife, or poison, or a rope. "Or should praise the beauty of death" means he shows the danger in living, and speaks in praise of dying. "Or should urge him on to death" means: bring a knife, or eat poison, or hang yourself with a rope and die. "Ho, man" is a term of address. "What good to you is this wretched, hard-lived life?" — life is called wretched: compared with the life of the rich, the life of the poor is wretched and base; compared with the life of the wealthy, the life of the poor is wretched; compared with the life of the gods, the life of humans is wretched. "Hard-lived" means: for one whose hands are cut off, whose feet are cut off, whose hands and feet are cut off, whose ears are cut off, whose nose is cut off, whose ears and nose are cut off — "with this wretched, this hard-lived life, death is better for you." "Thus the mind" means: what is mind is thought, what is thought is mind. "Mental intention" means: perceiving death, intending death, aiming at death. "In many ways" means in various manners. "Or should praise the beauty of death" means he shows the danger in living and speaks in praise of dying: "Having died from here, with the break-up of the body after death you will be reborn in a good destination, a heavenly world; there, furnished and endowed with the five divine strands of sensual pleasure, you will take your delight." "Or should urge him on to death" means: bring a knife, or eat poison, or hang yourself with a rope and die, or leap into a pit or a ravine. "This too" is said in reference to what precedes. "Becomes one who is defeated" — just as a great boulder split in two can never be rejoined, so too a bhikkhu who intentionally deprives a human being of life is no longer a recluse, no longer a son of the Sakyan. Therefore it is said: "he becomes one who is defeated." "Not in communion" — non-communion means: a single formal act, a single recitation, an equal training; that is what is called communion. That does not exist between him and [the Saṅgha]; therefore it is said "not in communion."

[173] Himself, by command, by a messenger, by a messenger in succession, by a messenger who goes astray, by a messenger who has gone and returned, [one who is] present [thinking] there is a hidden [one], [one who is] hidden [thinking] there is a present [one], present [knowing] present, hidden [knowing] hidden; he praises by body, praises by speech, praises by body and speech, praises by a messenger, praises by a letter; a pit-trap, a leaning-support, placing a weapon nearby, medicine, presenting a visible form, presenting a sound, presenting a smell, presenting a taste, presenting a touch, presenting a mental object; telling, instructing, making an appointed sign, making a signal.

[174] "Himself" means he kills by himself, with the body or with something attached to the body or with something cast [at the victim]. "By command" means having resolved upon it he orders: "Strike thus, hit thus, kill thus." A bhikkhu orders a bhikkhu: "Deprive so-and-so of life" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. He, thinking it is that one, deprives that one of life — there is an offence entailing defeat for both. A bhikkhu orders a bhikkhu: "Deprive so-and-so of life" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. He, thinking it is that one, deprives another of life — there is no offence for the original instigator. There is an offence entailing defeat for the killer. A bhikkhu orders a bhikkhu: "Deprive so-and-so of life" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. He, thinking it is another, deprives that one of life — there is an offence entailing defeat for both. A bhikkhu orders a bhikkhu: "Deprive so-and-so of life" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. He, thinking it is another, deprives another of life — there is no offence for the original instigator; there is an offence entailing defeat for the killer. A bhikkhu orders a bhikkhu: "Tell so-and-so: 'So-and-so says to tell so-and-so: So-and-so should deprive so-and-so of life'" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. He tells the other — there is an offence of wrong-doing. The killer accepts — there is an offence of grave offence for the original instigator. He deprives that one of life — there is an offence entailing defeat for all. A bhikkhu orders a bhikkhu: "Tell so-and-so: 'So-and-so says to tell so-and-so: So-and-so should deprive so-and-so of life'" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. He orders another — there is an offence of wrong-doing. The killer accepts — there is an offence of wrong-doing. He deprives that one of life — there is no offence for the original instigator; there is an offence entailing defeat for the one who ordered and for the killer. A bhikkhu orders a bhikkhu: "Deprive so-and-so of life" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. He goes and then comes back: "I am not able to deprive that one of life." He orders again: "When you are able, then deprive that one of life" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. He deprives that one of life — there is an offence entailing defeat for both. A bhikkhu orders a bhikkhu: "Deprive so-and-so of life" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Having ordered it, feeling remorse he does not announce: "Do not kill." He deprives that one of life — there is an offence entailing defeat for both. A bhikkhu orders a bhikkhu: "Deprive so-and-so of life" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Having ordered it, feeling remorse he announces: "Do not kill." He, [thinking] "I was ordered by you," deprives that one of life — there is no offence for the original instigator. There is an offence entailing defeat for the killer. A bhikkhu orders a bhikkhu: "Deprive so-and-so of life" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Having ordered it, feeling remorse he announces: "Do not kill." He desists, [saying] "Good" — there is no offence for either.

[175] Present, [thinking] there is a hidden one, he calls out: "Oh, that so-and-so would be killed" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Hidden, [thinking] there is a present one, he calls out: "Oh, that so-and-so would be killed" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Present, [knowing] present, he calls out: "Oh, that so-and-so would be killed" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Hidden, [knowing] hidden, he calls out: "Oh, that so-and-so would be killed" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. "He praises by body" means he makes a gesture with the body: "Whoever dies thus gains wealth, or gains fame, or goes to heaven" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Through that praise he gives rise to painful feeling, [thinking] "I shall die" — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. "He praises by speech" means he speaks with speech: "Whoever dies thus gains wealth, or gains fame, or goes to heaven" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Through that praise he gives rise to painful feeling, [thinking] "I shall die" — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. "He praises by body and speech" means he makes a gesture with the body and speaks with speech: "Whoever dies thus gains wealth, or gains fame, or goes to heaven" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Through that praise he gives rise to painful feeling, [thinking] "I shall die" — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. "He praises by a messenger" means he announces the messenger's message: "Whoever dies thus gains wealth, or gains fame, or goes to heaven" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Having heard the messenger's message, he gives rise to painful feeling, [thinking] "I shall die" — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat.

[176] "He praises by a letter" means he inscribes a letter: "Whoever dies thus gains wealth, or gains fame, or goes to heaven" — for each letter there is an offence of wrong-doing. Having seen the letter, he gives rise to painful feeling, [thinking] "I shall die" — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. "A pit-trap" means he digs a pit-trap intending a human being: "Having fallen in, he will die" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. When he has fallen in, painful feeling arises — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. He digs a pit-trap without intending [anyone in particular]: "Whoever falls in will die" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. A human being falls in there — there is an offence of wrong-doing. When he has fallen in, painful feeling arises — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. A yakkha, or a peta, or an animal in human form falls in there — there is an offence of wrong-doing. When it has fallen in, painful feeling arises — there is an offence of wrong-doing. It dies — there is an offence of grave offence. An animal falls in there — there is an offence of wrong-doing. When it has fallen in, painful feeling arises — there is an offence of wrong-doing. It dies — there is an offence of expiation.

[177] "A leaning-support" means on a support he places a knife, or smears it with poison, or makes it weak, or places it at a pit or a ravine or a precipice: "Having fallen, he will die" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. By the knife, or the poison, or the falling, painful feeling arises — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. "Placing a weapon nearby" means he places nearby a sword, or a spear, or a club, or a cudgel, or a stone, or a knife, or poison, or a rope: "By this he will die" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. [Thinking] "By that I shall die," he gives rise to painful feeling — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. "Medicine" means he gives ghee, or fresh butter, or oil, or honey, or molasses: "Having tasted this, he will die" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. When he has tasted it, painful feeling arises — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat.

[178] "Presenting a visible form" means he brings before him an unpleasant form, frightful and terrifying: "Having seen this, he will be frightened and die" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Having seen it, he is frightened — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. He brings before him a pleasant form: "Having seen this, pining away through not obtaining it, he will die" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Having seen it, he pines away through not obtaining it — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. "Presenting a sound" means he brings before him an unpleasant sound, frightful and terrifying: "Having heard this, he will be frightened and die" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Having heard it, he is frightened — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. He brings before him a pleasant sound, lovely and heart-captivating: "Having heard this, pining away through not obtaining it, he will die" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Having heard it, he pines away through not obtaining it — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. "Presenting a smell" means he brings before him an unpleasant smell, loathsome and repulsive: "Having smelt this, from loathsomeness and repulsion he will die" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. When he has smelt it, from loathsomeness and repulsion painful feeling arises — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. He brings before him a pleasant smell: "Having smelt this, pining away through not obtaining it, he will die" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Having smelt it, he pines away through not obtaining it — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. "Presenting a taste" means he brings before him an unpleasant taste, loathsome and repulsive: "Having tasted this, from loathsomeness and repulsion he will die" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. When he has tasted it, from loathsomeness and repulsion painful feeling arises — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. He brings before him a pleasant taste: "Having tasted this, pining away through not obtaining it, he will die" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Having tasted it, he pines away through not obtaining it — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. "Presenting a touch" means he brings before him an unpleasant touch, a painful contact, a harsh contact: "Touched by this, he will die" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Touched by it, painful feeling arises — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. He brings before him a pleasant touch, a pleasant contact, a soft contact: "Touched by this, pining away through not obtaining it, he will die" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Touched by it, he pines away through not obtaining it — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. "Presenting a mental object" means to one bound for hell he speaks talk of hell: "Having heard this, he will be frightened and die" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Having heard it, he is frightened — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. To one of good deeds he speaks talk of heaven: "Having heard this, resolved upon it, he will die" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Having heard it, resolved, he gives rise to painful feeling, [thinking] "I shall die" — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat.

[179] "Telling" means, when asked, he speaks: "Die thus. Whoever dies thus gains wealth, or gains fame, or goes to heaven" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Through that telling he gives rise to painful feeling, [thinking] "I shall die" — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. "Instructing" means, unasked, he speaks: "Die thus. Whoever dies thus gains wealth, or gains fame, or goes to heaven" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. Through that instructing he gives rise to painful feeling, [thinking] "I shall die" — there is an offence of grave offence. He dies — there is an offence entailing defeat. "Making an appointed sign" means he fixes an appointed sign — before the meal or after the meal, by night or by day: "By that appointed sign, deprive that one of life" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. By that appointed sign he deprives that one of life — there is an offence entailing defeat for both. If he deprives that one of life before or after that appointed sign, there is no offence for the original instigator; there is an offence entailing defeat for the killer. "Making a signal" means he makes a signal: "I shall wink an eye, or raise an eyebrow, or raise my head — by that signal, deprive that one of life" — there is an offence of wrong-doing. By that signal he deprives that one of life — there is an offence entailing defeat for both. If he deprives that one of life before or after that signal, there is no offence for the original instigator; there is an offence entailing defeat for the killer. There is no offence if it is unintentional, if he did not know, if he had no intention of causing death, if he is deranged, if he is the first offender.
