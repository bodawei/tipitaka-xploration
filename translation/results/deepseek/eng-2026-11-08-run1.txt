Pārājikapāḷi — 1. Pārājikakaṇḍaṃ
vin01m.mul.xml, verses 215

[215] If, intending to say "I enter the first jhāna by three modes," he deliberately speaks falsely saying "I enter the second jhāna," then for one who knows it to be false there is an offence entailing defeat; for one who does not know it to be false there is a grave offence. If, intending to say "I enter the first jhāna by three modes," he deliberately speaks falsely saying "I enter the third jhāna," then for one who knows it to be false there is an offence entailing defeat; for one who does not know it to be false there is a grave offence. If, intending to say "I enter the first jhāna by three modes," he deliberately speaks falsely saying "I enter the fourth jhāna," then for one who knows it to be false there is an offence entailing defeat; for one who does not know it to be false there is a grave offence. If, intending to say "I enter the first jhāna by three modes … up to seven modes," he deliberately speaks falsely saying "I enter the empty liberation … the signless liberation … the undirected liberation … empty concentration … signless concentration … undirected concentration … empty attainment … signless attainment … undirected attainment … the three true knowledges … the four foundations of mindfulness … the four right strivings … the four bases of psychic power … the five faculties … the five powers … the seven factors of enlightenment … the noble eightfold path … the fruit of stream-entry … the fruit of once-returning … the fruit of non-returning … arahantship," or "lust has been abandoned by me … hatred has been abandoned by me … delusion has been abandoned by me, spewed out, released, given up, renounced, cast off, utterly cast off," or "my mind is unobstructed by lust … my mind is unobstructed by hatred … my mind is unobstructed by delusion," then for one who knows it to be false there is an offence entailing defeat; for one who does not know it to be false there is a grave offence. Before speaking, it occurs to him: "I shall speak falsely"; while speaking, it occurs to him: "I am speaking falsely"; after speaking, it occurs to him: "I have spoken falsely" — he misrepresents his view, he misrepresents his belief, he misrepresents his preference, he misrepresents his state.
