Pārājikapāḷi — 1. Pārājikakaṇḍaṃ
vin01m.mul.xml, section verses 208-209 (condensed prose summary)

Anyone who deliberately lies, claiming to have attained and mastered the first jhāna in combination with the second, third, or fourth jhāna, or with the empty, signless, or undirected liberations, concentrations, and attainments, or with the three true knowledges, the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right strivings, the four bases of psychic power, the five faculties, the five powers, the seven factors of enlightenment, the noble eightfold path, or the fruits of stream-entry, once-returning, non-returning, and arahantship — or who claims that craving, hatred, and delusion have been abandoned, spewed out, released, given up, renounced, cast off, and utterly cast off in him, or that his mind is unobstructed by craving, hatred, and delusion — commits a pārājika offence. The same holds whether the claim is framed in three ways or up to seven ways, whether it concerns the first jhāna alone or in conjunction with any of these states. The full offence is incurred when, having misrepresented his view, belief, preference, or state, he intends beforehand to lie, lies, and has lied.
