Pārājikapāḷi — 1. Pārājikakaṇḍaṃ
vin01m.mul.xml, section verses 210-213 (condensed prose summary)

The fourth pārājika offence is then analysed in exhaustive detail. A bhikkhu who, with deliberate lying, claims to have entered, to enter, or to be entered into the second and third jhānas, or to be one who has obtained and mastered them, or that they have been realized by him, commits a pārājika. The same applies to pairings of the second jhāna with the fourth, and to claims of attainment joined with the empty, signless and undirected liberations, concentrations and attainments, with the three true knowledges, the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right strivings, the four bases of psychic power, the five faculties, the five powers, the seven factors of enlightenment, the noble eightfold path, and the fruits of stream-entry, once-returning, non-returning and arahantship. The formula is then repeated for each jhāna as base, each combined with each of the other attainments and with the stock declaration that greed, hatred and delusion have been abandoned, spewed out, released, given up, renounced, cast off and utterly cast off, and that his mind is unobstructed by them. In every case the claim may be made in the three modes—as one who has obtained, as master of it, as having realized it—and the offence is incurred when the words are spoken as deliberate lying. The threefold examination of each claim yields a pārājika for one who misrepresents his view, his belief, his preference or his state. The whole series is to be worked through with each jhāna and each attainment as base, combined with every other, rotating as a wheel, until pairs of every possible combination have been covered.
