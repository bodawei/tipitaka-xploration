Pārājikapāḷi — 1. Pārājikakaṇḍaṃ
vin01m.mul.xml, verses 97-106

[97] "Suspended aloft" means goods that are up in the air. They may be hung on a bed, or a chair, or a robe-pole, or a robe-line, or a wall-peg, or an elephant-tusk peg, or a tree, or even on a bowl-stand. Thinking, "I will steal goods suspended aloft," with thievish mind he looks for an accomplice or goes: an offence of wrong-doing. He touches it: an offence of wrong-doing. He makes it shake: a grave offence. He shifts it from its place: an offence entailing defeat.

[98] "Standing in water" means goods placed in water. Thinking, "I will steal goods standing in water," with thievish mind he looks for an accomplice or goes: an offence of wrong-doing. He dives in or surfaces: an offence of wrong-doing. He touches it: an offence of wrong-doing. He makes it shake: a grave offence. He shifts it from its place: an offence entailing defeat. If, with thievish mind, he touches a blue lotus, or a red lotus, or a white lotus, or a lotus root, or a fish, or a turtle growing there, worth five māsakas or more than five māsakas: an offence of wrong-doing. He makes it shake: a grave offence. He shifts it from its place: an offence entailing defeat.

[99] "Boat" means that by which one crosses over. "Standing in a boat" means goods placed in a boat. Thinking, "I will steal goods standing in a boat," with thievish mind he looks for an accomplice or goes: an offence of wrong-doing. He touches it: an offence of wrong-doing. He makes it shake: a grave offence. He shifts it from its place: an offence entailing defeat. Thinking, "I will steal the boat," with thievish mind he looks for an accomplice or goes: an offence of wrong-doing. He touches it: an offence of wrong-doing. He makes it shake: a grave offence. He unties the mooring: an offence of wrong-doing. Having untied the mooring, he touches it: an offence of wrong-doing. He makes it shake: a grave offence. He moves it upstream, or downstream, or across, even by so much as a hair's breadth: an offence entailing defeat.

Thinking, "I will steal the vehicle," with thievish mind he looks for an accomplice or goes: an offence of wrong-doing. He touches it: an offence of wrong-doing. He makes it shake: a grave offence. He shifts it from its place: an offence entailing defeat.

[101] "Load" means a load on the head, a load on the shoulder, a load on the hip, a load carried hanging in the hand. If with thievish mind he touches a load on the head: an offence of wrong-doing. He makes it shake: a grave offence. He lowers it to the shoulder: an offence entailing defeat. If with thievish mind he touches a load on the shoulder: an offence of wrong-doing. He makes it shake: a grave offence. He lowers it to the hip: an offence entailing defeat. If with thievish mind he touches a load on the hip: an offence of wrong-doing. He makes it shake: a grave offence. He takes it with the hand: an offence entailing defeat. If with thievish mind he puts down on the ground a load that is in the hand: an offence entailing defeat. If with thievish mind he takes it up from the ground: an offence entailing defeat.

[102] "Park" means a flower park, a fruit park. "Standing in a park" means goods placed in a park in any of four positions: resting on the ground, resting on a surface, standing in the open air, suspended aloft. Thinking, "I will steal goods standing in a park," with thievish mind he looks for an accomplice or goes: an offence of wrong-doing. He touches it: an offence of wrong-doing. He makes it shake: a grave offence. He shifts it from its place: an offence entailing defeat. If with thievish mind he touches a root, or bark, or a leaf, or a flower, or a fruit growing there, worth five māsakas or more than five māsakas: an offence of wrong-doing. He makes it shake: a grave offence. He shifts it from its place: an offence entailing defeat. He lays claim to the park: an offence of wrong-doing. He arouses doubt in the owner: a grave offence. When the owner, thinking, "It will not be mine," gives up his claim: an offence entailing defeat. If, going to law, he defeats the owner: an offence entailing defeat. If, going to law, he is himself defeated: a grave offence.

He shifts it from its place: an offence entailing defeat. He lays claim to the field: an offence of wrong-doing. He arouses doubt in the owner: a grave offence. When the owner, thinking, "It will not be mine," gives up his claim: an offence entailing defeat. If, going to law, he defeats the owner: an offence entailing defeat. If, going to law, he is himself defeated: a grave offence. If he shifts a stake, or a cord, or a fence, or a boundary marker: an offence of wrong-doing. While one further act remains to be done: a grave offence. When that act has been done: an offence entailing defeat.
