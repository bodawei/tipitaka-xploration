Pārājikapāḷi — 1. Pārājikakaṇḍaṃ

[200] For one who, speaking a deliberate lie, says "I attained the first jhāna," there is an offence entailing defeat in three ways: before speaking, it occurs to him, "I shall speak a falsehood"; while speaking, it occurs to him, "I am speaking a falsehood"; after speaking, it occurs to him, "a falsehood has been spoken by me." For one who, speaking a deliberate lie, says "I attained the first jhāna," there is an offence entailing defeat in four ways: before speaking, it occurs to him, "I shall speak a falsehood"; while speaking, it occurs to him, "I am speaking a falsehood"; after speaking, it occurs to him, "a falsehood has been spoken by me"; and he misrepresents his view. For one who, speaking a deliberate lie, says "I attained the first jhāna," there is an offence entailing defeat in five ways: before speaking, it occurs to him, "I shall speak a falsehood"; while speaking, it occurs to him, "I am speaking a falsehood"; after speaking, it occurs to him, "a falsehood has been spoken by me"; he misrepresents his view; he misrepresents his belief. For one who, speaking a deliberate lie, says "I attained the first jhāna," there is an offence entailing defeat in six ways: before speaking, it occurs to him, "I shall speak a falsehood"; while speaking, it occurs to him, "I am speaking a falsehood"; after speaking, it occurs to him, "a falsehood has been spoken by me"; he misrepresents his view; he misrepresents his belief; he misrepresents his preference.
