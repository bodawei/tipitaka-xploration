Pārājikapāḷi — 1. Pārājikakaṇḍaṃ

[190] Now at that time the group-of-six bhikkhus set fire to a jungle tract; people were burnt and died. They were remorseful… (and so on)… "There is no offence, bhikkhus, for one who had no intention of causing death." Now at that time the group-of-six bhikkhus, with the intention of causing death, set fire to a jungle tract. People were burnt and died… (and so on)… people were burnt but did not die. They were remorseful… (and so on)… "There is no offence entailing defeat, bhikkhus; it is a grave offence."

[191] Now at that time a certain bhikkhu went to the execution ground and said this to the executioner of thieves: "Friend, do not torment this man. Deprive him of life with a single blow." "Very well, venerable sir," he said, and deprived him of life with a single blow. That bhikkhu was remorseful… (and so on)… "Bhikkhu, you have fallen into an offence entailing defeat." Now at that time a certain bhikkhu went to the execution ground and said this to the executioner of thieves: "Friend, do not torment this man. Deprive him of life with a single blow." He said, "I will not do as you say," and deprived him of life. That bhikkhu was remorseful… (and so on)… "There is no offence entailing defeat, bhikkhu; it is an offence of wrong-doing."

[192] Now at that time a certain man whose hands and feet had been cut off was lying in the house of his relatives, surrounded by his kinsfolk. A certain bhikkhu said this to those people: "Friends, do you wish for this man's death?" "Yes, venerable sir, we do." "Then give him buttermilk to drink." They gave him buttermilk to drink, and he died. That bhikkhu was remorseful… (and so on)… "Bhikkhu, you have fallen into an offence entailing defeat." Now at that time a certain man whose hands and feet had been cut off was lying in a family house, surrounded by his kinsfolk. A certain bhikkhunī said this to those people: "Friends, do you wish for this man's death?" "Yes, noble lady, we do." "Then give him salted sour gruel to drink." They gave him salted sour gruel to drink, and he died. She was remorseful. Then that bhikkhunī told this matter to the bhikkhunīs. The bhikkhunīs told this matter to the bhikkhus. The bhikkhus told this matter to the Blessed One. "Bhikkhus, that bhikkhunī has fallen into an offence entailing defeat."

[193] At that time the Buddha, the Blessed One, was staying at Vesālī in the Great Wood, in the hall with the peaked roof. Now at that time a number of bhikkhus who were acquaintances and companions entered upon the rains residence on the bank of the river Vaggumudā. Now at that time there was a famine in the Vajjī country: it was hard to get a living, the crops were blighted and turned to straw, and food was doled out by ticket; it was not easy to keep oneself going by gleaning and favours. Then it occurred to those bhikkhus: "At present there is a famine in the Vajjī country: it is hard to get a living, the crops are blighted and turned to straw, and food is doled out by ticket; it is not easy to keep oneself going by gleaning and favours. Now by what means might we spend the rains comfortably, in concord, on friendly terms, without disputes, and not be troubled for almsfood?" Some spoke thus: "Come, friends, let us undertake work for the householders; then they will think fit to give to us. Thus we shall spend the rains comfortably, in concord, on friendly terms, without disputes, and shall not be troubled for almsfood." Some spoke thus: "Enough, friends, what is the good of undertaking work for the householders! Come, friends, let us carry messages for the householders; then they will think fit to give to us. Thus we shall spend the rains comfortably, in concord, on friendly terms, without disputes, and shall not be troubled for almsfood." Some spoke thus: "Enough, friends, what is the good of undertaking work for the householders! What is the good of carrying messages for the householders! Come, friends, let us speak to the householders in praise of one another's superhuman states: 'Such and such a bhikkhu is one who gains the first jhāna, such and such a bhikkhu is one who gains the second jhāna, such and such a bhikkhu is one who gains the third jhāna, such and such a bhikkhu is one who gains the fourth jhāna, such and such a bhikkhu is a stream-enterer, such and such a bhikkhu is a once-returner, such and such a bhikkhu is a non-returner, such and such a bhikkhu is an arahant, such and such a bhikkhu has the three true knowledges, such and such a bhikkhu has the six direct knowledges.' Then they will think fit to give to us. Thus we shall spend the rains comfortably, in concord, on friendly terms, without disputes, and shall not be troubled for almsfood." "This indeed is the better course, friends: that we speak to the householders in praise of one another's superhuman states."

[194] Then those bhikkhus spoke to the householders in praise of one another's superhuman states: "Such and such a bhikkhu is one who gains the first jhāna… (and so on)… such and such a bhikkhu has the six direct knowledges." Then those people thought: "It is indeed a gain for us, it is well gained by us, that such bhikkhus as these have entered upon the rains with us; never before have such bhikkhus entered upon the rains with us as these bhikkhus are, virtuous and of lovely character." And they did not themselves consume such food, nor give it to their mothers and fathers, nor to their wives and children, nor to their slaves, workers and servants, nor to their friends and colleagues, nor to their kinsmen and blood-relations, as they gave to the bhikkhus. They did not themselves eat, taste, or drink such foods of the hard kind, such delicacies and such drinks, nor give them to their mothers and fathers, nor to their wives and children, nor to their slaves, workers and servants, nor to their friends and colleagues, nor to their kinsmen and blood-relations, as they gave to the bhikkhus. So those bhikkhus became handsome, with plump faculties, with clear complexions and bright skin. Now it is the custom for bhikkhus who have completed the rains to go to see the Blessed One. So those bhikkhus, when the rains were completed and the three months had gone by, put their lodgings in order, took bowl and robe, and set out for Vesālī. Travelling by stages they came to Vesālī, to the Great Wood, to the hall with the peaked roof, and approached the Blessed One; having approached and paid homage to the Blessed One, they sat down to one side. Now at that time the bhikkhus who had completed the rains in the various districts were thin, rough, of poor complexion, grown very sallow, their limbs showing the veins; but the bhikkhus from the bank of the Vaggumudā were handsome, with plump faculties, with clear complexions and bright skin. Now it is the custom of the Buddhas, the Blessed Ones, to greet incoming bhikkhus. So the Blessed One said this to the bhikkhus from the bank of the Vaggumudā: "I hope, bhikkhus, that you are bearing up, that you are keeping going; I hope you spent the rains comfortably, in concord, on friendly terms, without disputes, and were not troubled for almsfood?" "We are bearing up, Blessed One, we are keeping going, Blessed One. We spent the rains comfortably, venerable sir, in concord, on friendly terms, without disputes, and were not troubled for almsfood." Tathāgatas ask knowing, and knowing they do not ask… (and so on)… for two reasons do the Buddhas, the Blessed Ones, question bhikkhus: "We shall teach the Dhamma," or "We shall lay down a training rule for the disciples." Then the Blessed One said this to the bhikkhus from the bank of the Vaggumudā: "But in what way, bhikkhus, did you spend the rains comfortably, in concord, on friendly terms, without disputes, and were not troubled for almsfood?" Then those bhikkhus told the matter to the Blessed One. "But was it true of you, bhikkhus?" "It was not true, Blessed One." The Buddha, the Blessed One, rebuked them: "It is unbecoming, foolish men, unsuitable, improper, unworthy of a recluse, not allowable, not to be done. How could you, foolish men, for the sake of your bellies speak to householders in praise of one another's superhuman states! It would be better for you, foolish men, that your bellies were slit open with a sharp butcher's knife than that for the sake of your bellies you should speak to householders in praise of one another's superhuman states! Why is that? Because from that cause, foolish men, you would incur death or deadly suffering, but you would not on that account, at the breaking up of the body after death, be reborn in a state of loss, a bad destination, the abyss, hell. But from this cause, foolish men, at the breaking up of the body after death you would be reborn in a state of loss, a bad destination, the abyss, hell. This, foolish men, is not for the confidence of those without confidence"… (and so on)… Having rebuked them and given a talk on Dhamma, he addressed the bhikkhus:

[195] "There are these five great thieves, bhikkhus, existing and found in the world. What five? Here, bhikkhus, it occurs to a certain great thief: 'When shall I, surrounded by a hundred or by a thousand, roam about in villages, towns and royal capitals, killing and causing to kill, cutting and causing to cut, burning and causing to burn!' And at a later time, surrounded by a hundred or by a thousand, he roams about in villages, towns and royal capitals, killing and causing to kill, cutting and causing to cut, burning and causing to burn. Just so, bhikkhus, it occurs here to a certain evil bhikkhu: 'When shall I, surrounded by a hundred or by a thousand, walk on tour in villages, towns and royal capitals, honoured, respected, revered, venerated and esteemed by householders and those gone forth alike, a receiver of robes, almsfood, lodgings and medicinal requisites for the sick!' And at a later time, surrounded by a hundred or by a thousand, he walks on tour in villages, towns and royal capitals, honoured, respected, revered, venerated and esteemed by householders and those gone forth alike, a receiver of robes, almsfood, lodgings and medicinal requisites for the sick. This, bhikkhus, is the first great thief existing and found in the world. "Again, bhikkhus, here a certain evil bhikkhu, having mastered the Dhamma and Discipline proclaimed by the Tathāgata, sets it down as his own. This, bhikkhus, is the second great thief existing and found in the world. "Again, bhikkhus, here a certain evil bhikkhu charges a pure person leading the holy life, one of pure conduct, with groundless unchastity. This, bhikkhus, is the third great thief existing and found in the world. "Again, bhikkhus, here a certain evil bhikkhu, with those things that are the heavy property and heavy requisites of the Saṅgha — that is to say, a monastery park, a site for a park, a dwelling, a site for a dwelling, a bed, a bench, a mattress, a pillow, a metal cauldron, a metal jar, a metal pitcher, a metal basin, an adze, an axe, a hatchet, a hoe, a spade, creepers, bamboo, muñja grass, babbaja grass, ordinary grass, clay, wooden goods, earthenware goods — wins over and ingratiates himself with householders. This, bhikkhus, is the fourth great thief existing and found in the world. "In this world with its deities, its Māras and its Brahmās, in this generation with its recluses and brahmins, its rulers and people, this is the chief great thief: he who lays claim to a superhuman state that is not real, not true. Why is that? Because, bhikkhus, you have eaten the country's almsfood by theft." Then the Blessed One, having rebuked those bhikkhus of the bank of the Vaggumudā in many ways for being hard to support, hard to maintain… (and so on)… "And thus, bhikkhus, you should recite this training rule: "Whatever bhikkhu, without directly knowing it, should claim of himself a superhuman state, knowledge and vision worthy of the noble ones, saying, 'Thus I know, thus I see,' and then at a later time, whether being cross-questioned or not being cross-questioned, having fallen into an offence and looking for purification, should say thus: 'Friends, not knowing I said I know, not seeing I said I see; I spoke what was empty and false' — he too is one defeated, not in communion." Thus this training rule was laid down by the Blessed One for the bhikkhus.

[196] Now at that time a number of bhikkhus, perceiving as seen what was not seen, perceiving as attained what was not attained, perceiving as reached what was not reached, perceiving as realized what was not realized, declared final knowledge through overestimation. At a later time their minds inclined to lust, their minds inclined to hatred, their minds inclined to delusion. They were remorseful: "A training rule has been laid down by the Blessed One. And we, perceiving as seen what was not seen, perceiving as attained what was not attained, perceiving as reached what was not reached, perceiving as realized what was not realized, have declared final knowledge through overestimation. Can it be that we have fallen into an offence entailing defeat?" They told this matter to the venerable Ānanda. The venerable Ānanda told this matter to the Blessed One. "There are, Ānanda, those bhikkhus who, perceiving as seen what was not seen, perceiving as attained what was not attained, perceiving as reached what was not reached, perceiving as realized what was not realized, declare final knowledge through overestimation. But that is not to be reckoned as an offence." "And thus, bhikkhus, you should recite this training rule:

[197] "Whatever bhikkhu, without directly knowing it, should claim of himself a superhuman state, knowledge and vision worthy of the noble ones, saying, 'Thus I know, thus I see,' and then at a later time, whether being cross-questioned or not being cross-questioned, having fallen into an offence and looking for purification, should say thus: 'Friends, not knowing I said I know, not seeing I said I see; I spoke what was empty and false' — except through overestimation, he too is one defeated, not in communion."

[198] Whatever means: whoever, of whatever sort… (and so on)… Bhikkhu means… (and so on)… this is the bhikkhu intended in this sense. Without directly knowing means: not knowing, not seeing in himself a wholesome state that is non-existent, unreal, not to be found, yet thinking, "There is a wholesome state in me." A superhuman state means: jhāna, liberation, concentration, attainment, knowledge and vision, development of the path, the realization of the fruits, the abandoning of defilements, the mind's freedom from hindrances, delight in an empty hut. Of himself means: he applies those wholesome states to himself, or he applies himself to those wholesome states. Knowledge means the three true knowledges. Vision: that which is knowledge is vision; that which is vision is knowledge. Should claim means: he should announce it to a woman or a man, to a householder or to one gone forth. "Thus I know, thus I see" means: "I know these states, I see these states; these states exist in me, and I am seen in these states." Then at a later time means: when the moment, the instant, the minute in which the claim was made has passed. Being cross-questioned means: being cross-questioned about the matter he has admitted: "What was attained by you? How was it attained by you? When was it attained by you? Where was it attained by you? Which defilements have been abandoned by you? Of which states are you a gainer?" Not being cross-questioned means: not being spoken to by anyone. Having fallen into an offence means: being of evil desires, overcome by desire, he has laid claim to a superhuman state that is not real, not true, and has fallen into an offence entailing defeat. Looking for purification means: wishing to become a householder, or wishing to become a lay follower, or wishing to become a monastery attendant, or wishing to become a novice. "Friends, not knowing I said I know, not seeing I said I see" means: "I do not know these states, I do not see these states; these states do not exist in me, and I am not seen in these states." "I spoke what was empty and false" means: "What I said was empty, what I said was false, what I said was untrue; I spoke without knowing." Except through overestimation means: setting aside overestimation. He too: this is said with reference to the preceding ones. Is one defeated means: just as a palm tree with its crown cut off is incapable of further growth, so too a bhikkhu of evil desires, overcome by desire, who lays claim to a superhuman state that is not real, not true, is not a recluse, not a son of the Sakyan. Therefore it is said, "He is one defeated." Not in communion: communion means a single formal act, a single recitation, an equal training — this is called communion. He has none of this with them. Therefore it is said, "not in communion."

[199] A superhuman state means: jhāna, liberation, concentration, attainment, knowledge and vision, development of the path, the realization of the fruits, the abandoning of defilements, the mind's freedom from hindrances, delight in an empty hut. Jhāna means: the first jhāna, the second jhāna, the third jhāna, the fourth jhāna. Liberation means: the empty liberation, the signless liberation, the undirected liberation. Concentration means: the empty concentration, the signless concentration, the undirected concentration. Attainment means: the empty attainment, the signless attainment, the undirected attainment. Knowledge and vision means the three true knowledges. Development of the path means: the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right efforts, the four bases of spiritual power, the five faculties, the five powers, the seven factors of enlightenment, the noble eightfold path. Realization of the fruits means: the realization of the fruit of stream-entry, the realization of the fruit of once-returning, the realization of the fruit of non-returning, the realization of arahantship. Abandoning of defilements means: the abandoning of lust, the abandoning of hatred, the abandoning of delusion. The mind's freedom from hindrances means: the mind's freedom from lust, the mind's freedom from hatred, the mind's freedom from delusion. Delight in an empty hut means: delight in an empty hut through the first jhāna, delight in an empty hut through the second jhāna, delight in an empty hut through the third jhāna, delight in an empty hut through the fourth jhāna.

[190] 爾時，六群比丘焚燒林野；有人被燒死。彼等心生追悔……乃至……「諸比丘，無致死之意圖者，無罪。」爾時，六群比丘以致死之意圖焚燒林野。有人被燒而死……乃至……有人被燒而未死。彼等心生追悔……乃至……「諸比丘，非波羅夷；犯偷蘭遮。」

[191] 爾時，有一比丘往刑場，對劊子手言：「友，勿令此人受苦。當以一擊奪其命。」「善哉，大德。」遂以一擊奪其命。彼比丘心生追悔……乃至……「比丘，汝犯波羅夷。」爾時，有一比丘往刑場，對劊子手言：「友，勿令此人受苦。當以一擊奪其命。」彼言：「我不依汝之言而作。」然仍奪其命。彼比丘心生追悔……乃至……「比丘，非波羅夷；犯突吉羅。」

[192] 爾時，有一男子手足被斷，在親屬家中為諸親屬所圍繞。有一比丘對彼等人言：「友，汝等欲此人死耶？」「是，大德，我等欲之。」「若然，當令彼飲酪漿。」彼等令彼飲酪漿。彼遂命終。彼比丘心生追悔……乃至……「比丘，汝犯波羅夷。」爾時，有一男子手足被斷，在俗家中為諸親屬所圍繞。有一比丘尼對彼等人言：「友，汝等欲此人死耶？」「是，聖姊，我等欲之。」「若然，當令彼飲鹽酸漿。」彼等令彼飲鹽酸漿。彼遂命終。彼比丘尼心生追悔。時，彼比丘尼以此事告諸比丘尼。諸比丘尼以此事告諸比丘。諸比丘以此事白世尊。「諸比丘，彼比丘尼犯波羅夷。」

[193] 爾時，佛世尊住毘舍離大林重閣講堂。爾時，眾多相識親厚之比丘於婆裘摩陀河岸邊入雨安居。爾時，跋耆國饑饉，難以維生，稻禾白枯，須憑籌分食，不易以乞食維持生活。時，彼諸比丘作是念：「今跋耆國饑饉，難以維生，稻禾白枯，須憑籌分食，不易以乞食維持生活。我等當以何方便，得和合歡喜無諍、安樂度雨安居，而不為乞食所苦？」有人如是言：「友，我等且為在家人執作事業，如是彼等當思施與我等。如是我等得和合歡喜無諍、安樂度雨安居，而不為乞食所苦。」有人如是言：「友，止！何須為在家人執作事業！友，我等且為在家人傳遞書信，如是彼等當思施與我等。如是我等得和合歡喜無諍、安樂度雨安居，而不為乞食所苦。」有人如是言：「友，止！何須為在家人執作事業！何須為在家人傳遞書信！友，我等且向在家人互相讚說彼此之上人法：『某比丘得初禪，某比丘得第二禪，某比丘得第三禪，某比丘得第四禪，某比丘是須陀洹，某比丘是斯陀含，某比丘是阿那含，某比丘是阿羅漢，某比丘得三明，某比丘得六神通。』如是彼等當思施與我等。如是我等得和合歡喜無諍、安樂度雨安居，而不為乞食所苦。」「友，此為最勝——即我等向在家人互相讚說彼此之上人法。」

[194] 於是彼諸比丘向在家人互相讚說彼此之上人法：「某比丘得初禪……乃至……某比丘得六神通。」時彼諸人言：「我等實有利得，我等實得善利，如是之比丘來我處入雨安居；我等從昔以來，未曾有如是持戒具善法之比丘來入雨安居。」彼等自己不食如是之食，亦不與父母、不與妻子、不與奴僕作人、不與朋友大臣、不與親族血親，而唯以如是之食施與諸比丘。彼等自己不噉、不嘗、不飲如是之嚼食、嘗食、飲物，亦不與父母、不與妻子、不與奴僕作人、不與朋友大臣、不與親族血親，而唯以如是者施與諸比丘。於是彼諸比丘容色光澤，諸根肥滿，面色和悅，膚色清淨。然諸比丘安居竟往詣世尊，乃是常法。於是彼諸比丘雨安居竟，過三月已，收拾坐臥具，執持衣缽，往毘舍離而去。次第至毘舍離大林重閣講堂，詣世尊所；至已，禮敬世尊，退坐一面。爾時，於諸方安居竟之諸比丘，形體羸瘦，粗陋憔悴，面色萎黃，筋脈畢露；而婆裘摩陀河岸之諸比丘容色光澤，諸根肥滿，面色和悅，膚色清淨。然諸佛世尊與客比丘共相問訊，乃是常法。於是世尊問婆裘摩陀河岸諸比丘言：「諸比丘，堪忍否？得過活否？汝等和合歡喜無諍、安樂度雨安居，而不為乞食所苦否？」「世尊，堪忍；世尊，得過活。大德，我等和合歡喜無諍、安樂度雨安居，而不為乞食所苦。」如來知而問，亦知而不問……乃至……諸佛世尊以二因緣問諸比丘：或將說法，或將為諸弟子制立學處。於是世尊問婆裘摩陀河岸諸比丘言：「諸比丘，汝等云何和合歡喜無諍、安樂度雨安居，而不為乞食所苦耶？」時彼諸比丘以此事白世尊。「諸比丘，汝等所說是真實否？」「世尊，非真實。」佛世尊呵責言：「愚癡人，此非所宜、非隨順、不相稱、非沙門法、不清淨、不應作。愚癡人，汝等云何為腹之故，向在家人互相讚說彼此之上人法！愚癡人，寧以利刃屠牛刀自剖其腹，不應為腹之故向在家人互相讚說彼此之上人法！何以故？愚癡人，由彼因緣，僅受死或等於死之苦，不致因此身壞命終後墮惡趣、惡生、險難處、地獄。然由此因緣，愚癡人，則身壞命終後將墮惡趣、惡生、險難處、地獄。愚癡人，此非令未信者生信……乃至……」呵責已，說法語已，告諸比丘言——

[195] 「諸比丘，世間有此五種大賊，實有存在。何等為五？諸比丘，此世有一大賊作是念：『我何時當為百人或千人所圍繞，遊行於村邑王都，殺、令殺，斷、令斷，燒、令燒！』彼於後時為百人或千人所圍繞，遊行於村邑王都，殺、令殺，斷、令斷，燒、令燒。諸比丘，如是，此世有一惡比丘作是念：『我何時當為百人或千人所圍繞，遊行於村邑王都，為在家出家所恭敬、尊重、崇仰、供養、禮敬，得衣服、飲食、床座、病緣醫藥資具！』彼於後時為百人或千人所圍繞，遊行於村邑王都，為在家出家所恭敬、尊重、崇仰、供養、禮敬，得衣服、飲食、床座、病緣醫藥資具。諸比丘，此是世間第一大賊，實有存在。「復次，諸比丘，此世有一惡比丘，學習如來所說之法與律，而據為己有。諸比丘，此是世間第二大賊，實有存在。「復次，諸比丘，此世有一惡比丘，以無根之非梵行誹謗清淨梵行者、行清淨梵行之人。諸比丘，此是世間第三大賊，實有存在。「復次，諸比丘，此世有一惡比丘，以僧伽之重物、重資具，即：園、園地、精舍、精舍地、床、椅、褥、枕、銅釜、銅甕、銅瓶、銅鍋、小斧、斧、鉞斧、鋤、鑿、蔓、竹、文闍草、波婆草、草、土、木器、陶器等，以此攝取、籠絡在家人。諸比丘，此是世間第四大賊，實有存在。「諸比丘，於含天、魔、梵之世間，於含沙門婆羅門、天人之眾生中，凡宣稱自己有不實不真之上人法者，此是最上之大賊。何以故？諸比丘，汝等以盜心受用國人之食。」於是世尊以種種方便呵責彼婆裘摩陀河岸諸比丘難養難滿……乃至……「諸比丘，當如是誦此學處——「若比丘，未證知而宣稱自身具上人法、足以成聖之智見，言：『我如是知，我如是見』，彼於後時，或被詰問、或不被詰問，既已犯罪而欲求清淨，如是言：『友，我實不知而言知，不見而言見。我所說空虛妄語。』此亦波羅夷，不共住。」世尊如是為諸比丘制立此學處。

[196] 爾時，眾多比丘於未見謂見想、未得謂得想、未證謂證想、未現證謂現證想，以增上慢而記說己之開悟。彼等於後時心趣於貪，心趣於瞋，心趣於癡。彼等心生追悔：「世尊已制學處。而我等於未見謂見想、未得謂得想、未證謂證想、未現證謂現證想，以增上慢記說己之開悟。我等豈非犯波羅夷罪耶？」彼等以此事告尊者阿難。尊者阿難以此事白世尊。「阿難，有諸比丘於未見謂見想、未得謂得想、未證謂證想、未現證謂現證想，以增上慢記說己之開悟。然此不成違犯。」「諸比丘，當如是誦此學處——

[197] 「若比丘，未證知而宣稱自身具上人法、足以成聖之智見，言：『我如是知，我如是見』，彼於後時，或被詰問、或不被詰問，既已犯罪而欲求清淨，如是言：『友，我實不知而言知，不見而言見。我所說空虛妄語。』除增上慢外，此亦波羅夷，不共住。」

[198] 「若」者，謂如是等……乃至……「比丘」者……乃至……於此義中，所謂比丘即指此。「未證知」者，謂於自身不實、不真、不存在之善法，不知不見，而言「我有善法」。上人法者，謂禪、解脫、三昧、等至、智見、修道、證果、斷煩惱、心離蓋、於空閑處之喜樂。「自身具」者，謂將彼等善法引至自身，或將自身引入彼等善法之中。「智」者，謂三明。「見」者，凡是智即是見，凡是見即是智。「宣稱」者，謂告白於女人、男人、在家人或出家人。「我如是知，我如是見」者，謂：我知此諸法，我見此諸法，此諸法存於我中，而我亦見於此諸法中。「於後時」者，謂於宣稱之剎那，過此剎那、此頃刻、此須臾之後。「被詰問」者，謂於其所自承之事而被詰問：「汝所證者何？汝云何證得？汝何時證得？汝於何處證得？汝斷何等煩惱？汝得何等法？」「不被詰問」者，謂無人對彼有所言說。「既已犯罪」者，謂惡欲、為欲所制，宣稱不實不真之上人法，而已犯波羅夷罪。「欲求清淨」者，謂欲作在家人，或欲作優婆塞，或欲作園民，或欲作沙彌。「友，我實不知而言知，不見而言見」者，謂：我不知此諸法，我不見此諸法，此諸法不存於我中，我亦不見於此諸法中。「我所說空虛妄語」者，謂：我所言空虛，我所言虛妄，我所言不實，我不知而言。「除增上慢外」者，謂除去增上慢。「此亦」者，乃就前者而說。「波羅夷」者，譬如多羅樹斷其頂端則不能再生，如是，比丘惡欲、為欲所制，宣稱不實不真之上人法，則非沙門、非釋子。故說「波羅夷」。「不共住」者，共住者，謂同一羯磨、同一說戒、同等學處——此名共住。彼與此無有共住，故說「不共住」。

[199] 上人法者，謂禪、解脫、三昧、等至、智見、修道、證果、斷煩惱、心離蓋、於空閑處之喜樂。「禪」者，謂初禪、第二禪、第三禪、第四禪。「解脫」者，謂空解脫、無相解脫、無願解脫。「三昧」者，謂空三昧、無相三昧、無願三昧。「等至」者，謂空等至、無相等至、無願等至。「智見」者，謂三明。「修道」者，謂四念處、四正勤、四神足、五根、五力、七覺支、八聖道。「證果」者，謂證須陀洹果、證斯陀含果、證阿那含果、證阿羅漢果。「斷煩惱」者，謂斷貪、斷瞋、斷癡。「心離蓋」者，謂心離貪、心離瞋、心離癡。「於空閑處之喜樂」者，謂以初禪於空閑處喜樂，以第二禪於空閑處喜樂，以第三禪於空閑處喜樂，以第四禪於空閑處喜樂。

Jānantāpi tathāgatā pucchanti, jānantāpi na pucchanti…pe… dvīhākārehi buddhā bhagavanto bhikkhū paṭipucchanti – dhammaṃ vā desessāma, sāvakānaṃ vā sikkhāpadaṃ paññāpessāmāti. Atha kho bhagavā vaggumudātīriye bhikkhū etadavoca – ‘‘yathā kathaṃ pana tumhe, bhikkhave, samaggā sammodamānā avivadamānā phāsukaṃ vassaṃ vasittha na ca piṇḍakena kilamitthā’’ti? Atha kho te bhikkhū bhagavato etamatthaṃ ārocesuṃ – ‘‘kacci pana vo, bhikkhave, bhūta’’nti? ‘‘Abhūtaṃ, bhagavā’’ti. Vigarahi buddho bhagavā – ‘‘ananucchavikaṃ, moghapurisā, ananulomikaṃ appatirūpaṃ assāmaṇakaṃ akappiyaṃ akaraṇīyaṃ. Kathañhi nāma tumhe, moghapurisā, udarassa kāraṇā gihīnaṃ aññamaññassa uttarimanussadhammassa vaṇṇaṃ bhāsissatha! Varaṃ tumhehi, moghapurisā, tiṇhena govikantanena kucchiṃ parikanto, na tveva udarassa kāraṇā gihīnaṃ aññamaññassa uttarimanussadhammassa vaṇṇo bhāsito! Taṃ kissa hetu? Tato nidānañhi, moghapurisā, maraṇaṃ vā nigaccheyya maraṇamattaṃ vā dukkhaṃ, na tveva tappaccayā kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapajjeya. Ito nidānañca kho, moghapurisā, kāyassa bhedā paraṃ maraṇā apāyaṃ duggatiṃ vinipātaṃ nirayaṃ upapajjeyya. Netaṃ, moghapurisā, appasannānaṃ vā pasādāya’’…pe… vigarahitvā dhammiṃ kathaṃ katvā bhikkhū āmantesi –

[195] ‘‘Pañcime, bhikkhave, mahācorā santo saṃvijjamānā lokasmiṃ. Katame pañca? Idha, bhikkhave, ekaccassa mahācorassa evaṃ hoti – ‘kudāssu nāmāhaṃ satena vā sahassena vā parivuto gāmanigamarājadhānīsu āhiṇḍissāmi hananto ghātento chindanto chedāpento pacanto pācento’ti! So aparena samayena satena vā sahassena vā parivuto gāmanigamarājadhānīsu āhiṇḍati hananto ghātento chindanto chedāpento pacanto pācento. Evameva kho, bhikkhave, idhekaccassa pāpabhikkhuno evaṃ hoti – ‘kudāssu nāmāhaṃ satena vā sahassena vā parivuto gāmanigamarājadhānīsu cārikaṃ carissāmi sakkato garukato mānito pūjito apacito gahaṭṭhānañceva pabbajitānañca, lābhī cīvarapiṇḍapātasenāsanagilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhārāna’nti! So aparena samayena satena vā sahassena vā parivuto gāmanigamarājadhānīsu cārikaṃ carati sakkato garukato mānito pūjito apacito gahaṭṭhānañceva pabbajitānañca, lābhī cīvarapiṇḍapātasenāsanagilānappaccayabhesajjaparikkhārānaṃ. Ayaṃ, bhikkhave, paṭhamo mahācoro santo saṃvijjamāno lokasmiṃ. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, idhekacco pāpabhikkhu tathāgatappaveditaṃ dhammavinayaṃ pariyāpuṇitvā attano dahati. Ayaṃ, bhikkhave, dutiyo mahācoro santo saṃvijjamāno lokasmiṃ. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, idhekacco pāpabhikkhu suddhaṃ brahmacāriṃ parisuddhaṃ brahmacariyaṃ carantaṃ amūlakena abrahmacariyena anuddhaṃseti. Ayaṃ, bhikkhave, tatiyo mahācoro santo saṃvijjamāno lokasmiṃ. ‘‘Puna caparaṃ, bhikkhave, idhekacco pāpabhikkhu yāni tāni saṅghassa garubhaṇḍāni garuparikkhārāni, seyyathidaṃ – ārāmo ārāmavatthu vihāro vihāravatthu mañco pīṭhaṃ bhisi bimbohanaṃ lohakumbhī lohabhāṇakaṃ lohavārako lohakaṭāhaṃ vāsī parasu kuṭhārī kudālo nikhādanaṃ valli veḷu muñjaṃ pabbajaṃ tiṇaṃ mattikā dārubhaṇḍaṃ mattikābhaṇḍaṃ, tehi gihīṃ saṅgaṇhāti upalāpeti. Ayaṃ, bhikkhave, catuttho mahācoro santo saṃvijjamāno lokasmiṃ. ‘‘Sadevake, bhikkhave, loke samārake sabrahmake sassamaṇabrāhmaṇiyā pajāya sadevamanussāya ayaṃ aggo mahācoro yo asantaṃ abhūtaṃ uttarimanussadhammaṃ ullapati. Taṃ kissa hetu? Theyyāya vo, bhikkhave, raṭṭhapiṇḍo bhutto’’ti. Atha kho bhagavā te vaggumudātīriye bhikkhū anekapariyāyena vigarahitvā dubbharatāya dupposatāya…pe… evañca pana, bhikkhave, imaṃ sikkhāpadaṃ uddiseyyātha – ‘‘Yo pana bhikkhu anabhijānaṃ uttarimanussadhammaṃ attupanāyikaṃ alamariyañāṇadassanaṃ samudācareyya – ‘iti jānāmi iti passāmī’ti, tato aparena samayena samanuggāhīyamāno vā asamanuggāhīyamāno vā āpanno visuddhāpekkho evaṃ vadeyya – ‘ajānamevaṃ, āvuso, avacaṃ jānāmi, apassaṃ passāmi. Tucchaṃ musā vilapi’nti, ayampi pārājiko hoti asaṃvāso’’ti. Evañcidaṃ bhagavatā bhikkhūnaṃ sikkhāpadaṃ paññattaṃ hoti.

[196] Tena kho pana samayena sambahulā bhikkhū adiṭṭhe diṭṭhasaññino apatte pattasaññino anadhigate adhigatasaññino asacchikate sacchikatasaññino adhimānena aññaṃ byākariṃsu. Tesaṃ aparena samayena rāgāyapi cittaṃ namati dosāyapi cittaṃ namati mohāyapi cittaṃ namati. Tesaṃ kukkuccaṃ ahosi – ‘‘bhagavatā sikkhāpadaṃ paññattaṃ. Mayañcamha adiṭṭhe diṭṭhasaññino apatte pattasaññino anadhigate adhigatasaññino asacchikate sacchikatasaññino, adhimānena aññaṃ byākarimhā. Kacci nu kho mayaṃ pārājikaṃ āpattiṃ āpannā’’ti? Te āyasmato ānandassa etamatthaṃ ārocesuṃ. Āyasmā ānando bhagavato etamatthaṃ ārocesi. ‘‘Honti ye te, ānanda , bhikkhū adiṭṭhe diṭṭhasaññino apatte pattasaññino anadhigate adhigatasaññino asacchikate sacchikatasaññino adhimānena aññaṃ byākaronti. Tañca kho etaṃ abbohārika’’nti. ‘‘Evañca pana, bhikkhave, imaṃ sikkhāpadaṃ uddiseyyātha –

[197] ‘‘Yo pana bhikkhu anabhijānaṃ uttarimanussadhammaṃ attupanāyikaṃ alamariyañāṇadassanaṃ samudācareyya – ‘iti jānāmi iti passāmī’ti, tato aparena samayena samanuggāhīyamāno vā asamanuggāhīyamāno vā āpanno visuddhāpekkho evaṃ vadeyya – ‘ajānamevaṃ, āvuso, avacaṃ jānāmi, apassaṃ passāmi. Tucchaṃ musā vilapi’nti, aññatra adhimānā, ayampi pārājiko hoti asaṃvāso’’ti.

[198] Yo panāti yo yādiso…pe… bhikkhūti…pe… ayaṃ imasmiṃ atthe adhippeto bhikkhūti. Anabhijānanti asantaṃ abhūtaṃ asaṃvijjamānaṃ ajānanto apassanto attani kusalaṃ dhammaṃ – atthi me kusalo dhammoti. Uttarimanussadhammo nāma jhānaṃ vimokkho samādhi samāpatti ñāṇadassanaṃ maggabhāvanā phalasacchikiriyā kilesappahānaṃ vinīvaraṇatā cittassa suññāgāre abhirati. Attupanāyikanti te vā kusale dhamme attani upaneti attānaṃ vā tesu kusalesu dhammesu upaneti. Ñāṇanti tisso vijjā. Dassananti yaṃ ñāṇaṃ taṃ dassanaṃ. Yaṃ dassanaṃ taṃ ñāṇaṃ. Samudācareyyāti āroceyya itthiyā vā purisassa vā gahaṭṭhassa vā pabbajitassa vā. Iti jānāmi iti passāmīti jānāmahaṃ ete dhamme, passāmahaṃ ete dhamme atthi ca ete dhammā mayi, ahañca etesu dhammesu sandissāmīti. Tato aparena samayenāti yasmiṃ khaṇe samudāciṇṇaṃ hoti taṃ khaṇaṃ taṃ layaṃ taṃ muhuttaṃ vītivatte. Samanuggāhīyamānoti yaṃ vatthu paṭiññātaṃ hoti tasmiṃ vatthusmiṃ samanuggāhīyamāno – ‘‘kinte adhigataṃ, kinti te adhigataṃ, kadā te adhigataṃ, kattha te adhigataṃ, katame te kilesā pahīnā, katamesaṃ tvaṃ dhammānaṃ lābhī’’ti. Asamanuggāhīyamānoti na kenaci vuccamāno. Āpannoti pāpiccho icchāpakato asantaṃ abhūtaṃ uttarimanussadhammaṃ ullapitvā pārājikaṃ āpattiṃ āpanno hoti. Visuddhāpekkhoti gihī vā hotukāmo upāsako vā hotukāmo ārāmiko vā hotukāmo sāmaṇero vā hotukāmo. Ajānamevaṃ, āvuso, avacaṃ – jānāmi, apassaṃ passāmīti nāhaṃ ete dhamme jānāmi, nāhaṃ ete dhamme passāmi, natthi ca ete dhammā mayi, na cāhaṃ etesu dhammesu sandissāmīti. Tucchaṃ musā vilapinti tucchakaṃ mayā bhaṇitaṃ, musā mayā bhaṇitaṃ, abhūtaṃ mayā bhaṇitaṃ, ajānantena mayā bhaṇitaṃ. Aññatra adhimānāti ṭhapetvā adhimānaṃ. Ayampīti purime upādāya vuccati. Pārājiko hotīti seyyathāpi nāma tālo matthakacchinno abhabbo puna virūḷhiyā, evameva bhikkhu pāpiccho icchāpakato asantaṃ abhūtaṃ uttarimanussadhammaṃ ullapitvā assamaṇo hoti asakyaputtiyo. Tena vuccati – ‘‘pārājiko hotī’’ti. Asaṃvāsoti saṃvāso nāma ekakammaṃ ekuddeso samasikkhatā – eso saṃvāso nāma. So tena saddhiṃ natthi. Tena vuccati – ‘‘asaṃvāso’’ti.

[199] Uttarimanussadhammo nāma jhānaṃ vimokkho samādhi samāpatti ñāṇadassanaṃ maggabhāvanā phalasacchikiriyā kilesappahānaṃ vinīvaraṇatā cittassa suññāgāre abhirati. Jhānanti paṭhamaṃ jhānaṃ dutiyaṃ jhānaṃ tatiyaṃ jhānaṃ catutthaṃ jhānaṃ. Vimokkhoti suññato vimokkho animitto vimokkho appaṇihito vimokkho. Samādhīti suññato samādhi animitto samādhi appaṇihito samādhi. Samāpattīti suññatā samāpatti animittā samāpatti appaṇihitā samāpatti. Ñāṇadassananti tisso vijjā. Maggabhāvanāti cattāro satipaṭṭhānā, cattāro sammappadhānā, cattāro iddhipādā, pañcindriyāni, pañca balāni, satta bojjhaṅgā, ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo. Phalasacchikiriyāti sotāpattiphalassa sacchikiriyā, sakadāgāmiphalassa sacchikiriyā, anāgāmiphalassa sacchikiriyā, arahattassa sacchikiriyā. Kilesappahānanti rāgassa pahānaṃ dosassa pahānaṃ mohassa pahānaṃ. Vinīvaraṇatā cittassāti rāgā cittaṃ vinīvaraṇatā, dosā cittaṃ vinīvaraṇatā, mohā cittaṃ vinīvaraṇatā. Suññāgāre abhiratīti paṭhamena jhānena suññāgāre abhirati, dutiyena jhānena suññāgāre abhirati, tatiyena jhānena suññāgāre abhirati, catutthena jhānena suññāgāre abhirati.
